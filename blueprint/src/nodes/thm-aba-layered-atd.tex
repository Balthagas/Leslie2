\begin{theorem}[The layered presentation is exact]
  \label{thm:aba-layered-atd}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.Layer.regG}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Layer.regC}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Layer.regA}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Layer.regroup}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Layer.layeredForward}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Layer.layeredConverse}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Layer.layeredSim}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Layer.layeredSimConverse}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Layer.layered_atd}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-layered, def:aba-procnet, def:aba-netadv, def:strongfunctional, def:achievable, thm:forward-sound}
  For all parameters $P$,
  \[
    \operatorname{ATD}(\mathrm{deployed}(P)) \;=\;
    \operatorname{ATD}(\mathrm{layered}(P)) :
  \]
  the deployed system and the same system cut along its layer boundaries achieve exactly the same trace distributions.
  Neither side is restricted, and the corruption budget enters the statement nowhere, being the guard on the $\mathrm{fail}$ row of the one component that owns the corrupted set on either side.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-layered, def:aba-procnet, def:aba-netadv, def:strongfunctional, def:achievable, thm:forward-sound}
  The regrouping \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.Layer.regroup} carries a deployed state to an layered one: the round-$r$ stage slices of the nodes and the adversary's round-$r$ pools make the round-$r$ subsystem, the nodes' coordinator slices make the round loops, the adversary's DECIDED pools make $\mathrm{aNet}(P)$, and the coin oracle is carried across untouched.
  Every rule matches guard for guard along it, each row splitting along the layer boundary into its core-slice half and its stage-slice half, each Byzantine drive keeping its authorisation at $\mathrm{aNet}(P)$ and its effect at the round subsystem (D11), and the hiding order being the same on both sides.
  No invariant and no reachability hypothesis enters, every copy of the corrupted set on the layered side being the adversary's set read along the map rather than a field that could drift from it.
  Every transition but the coin resolution is Dirac and a transition and its image have the same successor, so Lemma~\ref{def:strongfunctional} gives a probabilistic forward simulation along the graph of the regrouping and a second along its converse; the map is not surjective, an layered state whose copies of the corrupted set disagree having no preimage, which is why the backward direction is the converse of the graph and not the graph of an inverse.
  Soundness (Theorem~\ref{thm:forward-sound}) turns each matching into an inclusion, and antisymmetry of $\subseteq$ gives the equality.
\end{proof}
