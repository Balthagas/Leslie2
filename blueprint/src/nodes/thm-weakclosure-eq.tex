\begin{theorem}[The weak closure preserves trace distributions]
  \label{thm:weakclosure-eq}
  \lean{PLTS.weakClosure_traceProb_eq}\leanok
  \uses{def:achievable, def:weakclosure}
  $\operatorname{ATD}(sys) = \operatorname{ATD}(sys^w)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{lem:map}
  The easy inclusion is Lemma~\ref{lem:map} with $f = \mathrm{id}$, every strong step of $sys$ being a degenerate weak step of $sys^w$, and it is \leandeclnamed{weakClosure_traceProb_subset}{PLTS.weakClosure_traceProb_subset}.
  The hard inclusion, \leandeclnamed{weakClosure_traceProb_superset}{PLTS.weakClosure_traceProb_superset}, turns a scheduler on $sys^w$ into a scheduler on $sys$ with the same trace distribution, by \algoref{alg:expand}{the expansion procedure}: each outer draw of a weak transition is realised by a witness scheduler (\leandeclnamed{Realises}{PLTS.Realises}), which is run to its halt before the outer scheduler is consulted again.
  The control flow is deterministic and the draws random, so each reachable configuration is reached with a well-defined probability (\leandeclnamed{reachProb}{PLTS.reachProb}), and the induced memoryless concrete scheduler (\leandeclnamed{expandSched}{PLTS.expandSched}) emits at a concrete history the Bayesian posterior over the next inner draw given that history.
  Fidelity (\leandeclnamed{traceProb_expandSched_eq}{PLTS.traceProb_expandSched_eq}) is proved configuration by configuration: the abstract side collects the entry configurations of a trace and the concrete side its arrival configurations, and the two are the same external weak step measured one moment apart.
  Their masses are equal rather than merely comparable, because a witness's post-$\tau$ run halts almost surely and no mass leaks to divergence inside it.
\end{proof}
