\begin{theorem}[The round subsystem simulation]
  \label{thm:aba-subsim}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.GSub.subDefl}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GSub.sub_projects}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GSub.gPull}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GSub.liftedSpecG}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GSub.Rsub}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GSub.subSim}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GSub.subSim_init}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GSub.subSim_failAct}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-gbcasub, def:aba-gbca-impl, def:aba-subspecs, thm:gbca-inst-refines, def:forwardsim}
  For all parameters $P$ and every round $r$, the round subsystem
  $\mathrm{sub}(P,r)$ is forward-simulated by the round-$r$ GBCA specification
  instance, read over the subsystem's interface.
  The relation is the instance relation of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:gbca-inst-refines}, read on the deflated state.
  It holds at the initial states and survives the corruption broadcast.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-gbcasub, def:aba-gbca-impl, def:aba-subspecs, thm:gbca-inst-refines, def:forwardsim, def:weakstep}
  The argument runs through the monolithic instance of
  Definition~\ref{def:aba-gbca-impl} in two legs, and reproves none of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:gbca-inst-refines}.

  The first leg is strong and functional.
  Define $\mathrm{subDefl}$ on a subsystem state by reading the programs'
  records as the instance's $proc$, their inbox rows as its $recv$, the
  fabric's pools as its $sent$ and the fabric's corrupted set as its $F$.
  The two state shapes hold the same four fields under a different partition,
  so the map is a bijection onto the instance's state space.
  Every transition of $\mathrm{sub}(P,r)$ is a transition of the instance at
  the deflated state, on the label the interface label projects to, with the
  successor pushed forward: one step for one step, with no stuttering.
  A joint call is the instance's call; a hidden multicast is the protocol rule
  it carries and a hidden delivery the instance's delivery; a fabric injection
  is the instance's Byzantine row; a drive is the instance's own row for the
  handshake it drives.

  The second leg is the projection of alphabets.
  The specification instance speaks $\mathrm{Lab}(n)$, the subsystem
  $\mathrm{NLab}(n)$, in which the drives and the call loop of round $r$ are
  separate labels.
  Let $\mathrm{gPull}$ be the partial map that identifies them with the
  specification labels they stand for: a driven call and either call loop are
  the call $\mathrm{callG}(r,\cdot,\cdot)$, a driven return is the return
  $\mathrm{retG}(r,\cdot,\cdot)$, a shared label is its own, and every other
  extended label has no image.
  The lifted specification $\mathrm{liftedSpecG}(P,r)$ is the specification
  instance read back along $\mathrm{gPull}$ by the construction
  $\mathrm{mapIdle}$ of Definition~\ref{def:aba-netadv}: a label with an image
  steps by that image's rows, and a label without one is a self-loop.
  Only $\tau$ projects to $\tau$, since every drive projects to a handshake
  port.

  Now compose the legs.
  A step of the subsystem on $l$ is a step of the instance on
  $\mathrm{gPull}(l)$, which Theorem~\ref{thm:gbca-inst-refines} answers by a
  weak transition of the specification instance.
  That answer is read back to the interface along the section of
  $\mathrm{gPull}$ that sends $\mathrm{gPull}(l)$ to $l$ itself and every other
  specification label to its own copy.
  This is where the specification's own call row answers a driven call and its
  own return row a driven return: the specification is input-enabled on those
  rows (D11), and the answer needs no rule of its own.
  A weak transition transports segment by segment under the section, because
  the silent segments are answered by silent segments and the visible label is
  answered by $l$.

  Two side conditions remain.
  The initial states are related, being the instance's own initial states under
  $\mathrm{subDefl}$.
  Corruption preserves the relation: the fabric's corrupted set is the
  instance's, so the two guards $k \notin F \wedge |F| < f$ are the same
  proposition and the instance-level statement applies verbatim (D1).
\end{proof}
