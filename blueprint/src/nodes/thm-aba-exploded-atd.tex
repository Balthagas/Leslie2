\begin{theorem}[The exploded presentation is exact]
  \label{thm:aba-exploded-atd}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.regG}\lean{PLTS.ABA.regC}\lean{PLTS.ABA.regA}\lean{PLTS.ABA.regroup}\lean{PLTS.ABA.explodedForward}\lean{PLTS.ABA.explodedConverse}\lean{PLTS.ABA.explodedSim}\lean{PLTS.ABA.explodedSimConverse}\lean{PLTS.ABA.exploded_atd}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-exploded, def:aba-procnet, def:aba-netadv, def:strongfunctional, def:achievable, thm:forward-sound}
  For all parameters $P$,
  \[
    \operatorname{ATD}(\mathrm{netFlat}(P)) \;=\;
    \operatorname{ATD}(\mathrm{exploded}(P)) :
  \]
  the $n$ corruption-blind programs beside the network adversary and the coin
  oracle, and the same system cut along its layer boundaries, achieve exactly
  the same trace distributions.
  Neither side is restricted.
  The corruption budget is the guard on the $\mathrm{fail}$ row of the one
  component that owns the corrupted set on either side, so it holds of every
  execution of either system by construction and enters the statement nowhere.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-exploded, def:aba-procnet, def:aba-netadv, def:strongfunctional, def:achievable, thm:forward-sound}
  Define the regrouping $\mathrm{regroup}$ from states of the deployed system
  to states of the exploded one.
  The round-$r$ subsystem takes its stage programs from the round-$r$ stage
  slices of the nodes and its fabric from the adversary's $pool(r,\cdot)$; the
  round loops take the nodes' coordinator slices; the ABA-side network takes
  the adversary's $dpool$; the coin oracle occupies the same coordinate on both
  sides and is carried across untouched.
  Every copy of the corrupted set on the exploded side is the adversary's one
  set $F$.
  The map carries the initial state to the initial state, every pool being
  empty and nobody corrupted there.

  Every rule matches guard for guard.
  A row of a deployed program splits along the layer boundary into its
  core-slice half, which is a row of $\mathrm{coreProcN}(P,j)$, and its
  stage-slice half, which is a row of a stage program; the composition joins
  them again, and the two rows the fusion of layers created --- the call and
  the graded return --- are exactly the ones with content on both sides.
  A row of the adversary splits the same way: the round-tagged pools and the
  injection $\mathrm{byzG}$ are a round fabric's, the DECIDED pools and the
  corrupted set are $\mathrm{aNet}(P)$'s.
  A stage multicast and a stage delivery are events internal to a round
  subsystem on the exploded side and hidden rendezvous of the deployed
  composition on the other; both are relabelled to $\tau$, and the hiding order
  is the same on both sides.
  A Byzantine drive matches because its authorisation $k \in F$ is read at
  $\mathrm{aNet}(P)$ off the very set the deployed adversary's guard reads
  (D11), while its effect is read at the round subsystem.
  The coin resolution appears on the two sides as the same pushforward of
  $\mathrm{wccPMF}$.
  Every transition of either system other than that resolution is Dirac, and a
  transition and its image have the same successor, so $\mathrm{regroup}$ both
  commutes with steps and reflects them.
  No reachability hypothesis and no invariant is needed for this, because every
  copy of the corrupted set on the exploded side is the adversary's set along
  the map rather than a separate field that could drift from it.

  Lemma~\ref{def:strongfunctional} therefore gives a probabilistic forward
  simulation along the graph of $\mathrm{regroup}$ and a second along its
  converse, neither system being an LTS.
  The map is not surjective --- an exploded state whose copies of the corrupted
  set disagree has no preimage --- which is why the backward direction is the
  converse of the graph and not the graph of an inverse.
  Soundness (Theorem~\ref{thm:forward-sound}) turns each matching into a
  trace-distribution inclusion, and antisymmetry of $\subseteq$ gives the
  equality.
\end{proof}
