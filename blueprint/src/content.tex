\section{Systems, executions, and trace distributions}

Throughout, $Label$ is a fixed label type carrying a distinguished \emph{silent}
label $\tau$ (the type class \texttt{Silent}). A label is \emph{internal} when
it equals $\tau$ and \emph{external} otherwise; this partition depends only on the
label type, not on any individual system.

Write $\delta_s$, or $\delta(s)$ when the argument is compound, for the Dirac
distribution on $s$.
Write $\mu \triangleright g$ for the pushforward of $\mu$ along $g$, and
$s[x := v]$ for record update.

\begin{definition}[Probabilistic labelled transition system]
  \label{def:system}
  \lean{PLTS.System}\leanok
  A \emph{PLTS} over a state type $State$ and label type $Label$ is a designated
  initial state $init : State$ together with a step relation
  $step : State \to Label \to \operatorname{PMF} State \to \mathrm{Prop}$. We write
  $s \xrightarrow{l} \mu$ for $step\ s\ l\ \mu$: from $s$, on label $l$, the system
  may move to the probability distribution $\mu$ over successor states.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Execution]
  \label{def:exec}
  \lean{PLTS.AlterSeq}\leanok
  An \emph{execution} is an alternating sequence $e = \langle init, trans\rangle$:
  an initial state followed by a possibly infinite sequence
  $trans : \operatorname{Seq}(Label \times State)$ of $(label, next\ state)$ pairs.
  Its \emph{trace} keeps only the external labels.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Trace]
  \label{def:trace}
  \lean{PLTS.System.trace}\leanok
  \uses{def:exec}
  The trace of an execution $e$ is the sequence of labels obtained from $trans$ by
  deleting every internal ($\tau$) transition and projecting to the label
  component. Executions that differ only by trailing $\tau$-transitions have the
  same trace.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Probabilistic execution and its path measure]
  \label{def:probexec}
  \lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticExecution}\leanok
  \uses{def:system, def:exec}
  A \emph{probabilistic execution} of $sys$ is an initial distribution
  $initState : \operatorname{PMF} State$ together with a \emph{scheduler} that, at
  each finite history, returns a sub-distribution over the next $(l, \mu)$ move (or
  halts). For a finite execution $e$ it induces a mass
  $probOf(e) \in [0, \infty]$
  (\lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticExecution.probOf}), the probability that the
  scheduler, started from $initState$, follows exactly the path $e$: the initial
  mass on $e.init$ times the product of the one-step kernels along $trans$.
\end{definition}

The trace probability collapses the path measure onto observable behaviour. To
avoid counting a single trajectory once for every trailing block of
$\tau$-transitions, we sum only over \emph{tight} executions.

\begin{definition}[Tight execution]
  \label{def:istight}
  \lean{PLTS.System.IsTight}\leanok
  \uses{def:exec}
  A finite execution is \emph{tight} if it has no transitions, or its last
  transition is external. Each trajectory has, for a given finite trace, a unique
  tight prefix: the shortest prefix with that trace (ending right after its last
  external transition).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Trace probability]
  \label{def:traceprob}
  \lean{PLTS.System.traceProb}\leanok
  \uses{def:probexec, def:trace, def:istight}
  For a probabilistic execution $pe$ and a finite trace $\sigma$, the trace
  probability $traceProb(pe, \sigma)$ is the sum of $probOf(e)$ over all tight
  finite executions $e$ with trace $\sigma$. Tightness makes the summands
  correspond to disjoint cylinders, so this is the standard trace-cone
  probability: the chance that the run's external labels begin with $\sigma$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Achievable trace distributions]
  \label{def:achievable}
  \lean{PLTS.achievableTraceDists}\leanok
  \uses{def:traceprob}
  The set $\operatorname{ATD}(sys)$ of \emph{achievable trace distributions} of
  $sys$ is the set of functions $\sigma \mapsto traceProb(pe, \sigma)$ obtained
  from probabilistic executions $pe$ whose initial distribution is the Dirac
  $\delta_{sys.init}$ (genuine runs from the designated start state). Two systems
  are trace-distribution equivalent when these sets coincide.
\end{definition}

The Dirac-init restriction is essential: it is exactly what makes the
distribution-monad construction below trace-preserving, and it is the semantics in
which all of the soundness statements are phrased.

\section{Weak transitions and the two constructions}

Weak transitions let internal ($\tau$) computation be absorbed. All three are
distribution-to-distribution, run-to-halt notions.

\begin{definition}[Silent weak transition]
  \label{def:weaktau}
  \lean{PLTS.weakTau}\leanok
  \uses{def:system}
  $weakTau\ sys\ \mu_0\ \mu$ holds when there is an internal-only scheduler that,
  started from $\mu_0$, halts almost surely and whose distribution of final states
  is $\mu$. Intuitively $\mu_0 \Rightarrow_\tau \mu$: run $\tau$-steps until
  spontaneous termination, and $\mu$ records where you stop.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Hyper-step]
  \label{def:hyperstep}
  \lean{PLTS.hyperStep}\leanok
  \uses{def:system}
  $hyperStep\ sys\ \mu_{\mathrm{pre}}\ l\ \mu_{\mathrm{post}}$ holds when
  $\mu_{\mathrm{post}}$ is a convex combination of one-step $l$-successors of the
  points of $\mu_{\mathrm{pre}}$: there is $p : State \to \operatorname{PMF}(\operatorname{PMF} State)$
  with $s \xrightarrow{l} \mu$ for every $s \in \operatorname{supp}\mu_{\mathrm{pre}}$
  and $\mu \in \operatorname{supp}(p\,s)$, and
  $\mu_{\mathrm{post}} = \mu_{\mathrm{pre}} \mathbin{\gg\!=} (s \mapsto (p\,s)\mathbin{\gg\!=}\mathrm{id})$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[External weak transition]
  \label{def:weakstep}
  \lean{PLTS.weakStep}\leanok
  \uses{def:weaktau, def:hyperstep}
  $weakStep\ sys\ \mu_0\ l\ \mu_f$ is a $\tau^\ast\!\cdot l\cdot\tau^\ast$ chain:
  there exist $\mu, \mu'$ with $\mu_0 \Rightarrow_\tau \mu$, a hyper-step
  $\mu \xrightarrow{l} \mu'$, and $\mu' \Rightarrow_\tau \mu_f$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Weak closure $sys^w$]
  \label{def:weakclosure}
  \lean{PLTS.System.weakClosure}\leanok
  \uses{def:weaktau, def:weakstep}
  The \emph{weak closure} $sys^w$ has the same state space and initial state as
  $sys$, but replaces its steps by weak transitions from a Dirac: for internal $l$,
  $s \xrightarrow{l} \mu$ iff $weakTau\ sys\ \delta_s\ \mu$; for external $l$,
  $s \xrightarrow{l} \mu$ iff $weakStep\ sys\ \delta_s\ l\ \mu$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Distribution-monad lift $\mathcal{D}(sys)$]
  \label{def:dist}
  \lean{PLTS.System.dist}\leanok
  \uses{def:hyperstep}
  The \emph{distribution-monad lift} $\mathcal{D}(sys)$ has state space
  $\operatorname{PMF} State$, initial state $\delta_{sys.init}$, and steps
  $\mu \xrightarrow{l} \omega$ (with $\omega : \operatorname{PMF}(\operatorname{PMF} State)$)
  exactly when $hyperStep\ sys\ \mu\ l\ (\omega \mathbin{\gg\!=}\mathrm{id})$. It
  packages ``a distribution over states'' as a single state.
\end{definition}

\section{Probabilistic simulations}

A simulation of a \emph{concrete} system $sys_C$ by an \emph{abstract} system
$sys_A$ is carried by a relation $R$ and a step-matching condition, phrased via a
coupling of the resulting distributions.

\begin{definition}[Coupling of distributions]
  \label{def:pmfrel}
  \lean{PLTS.PMFRel}\leanok
  For $R : \alpha \to \beta \to \mathrm{Prop}$, the lifted relation
  $\operatorname{PMFRel} R\ \mu_1\ \mu_2$ holds when there is a joint distribution
  $\omega$ on $\alpha \times \beta$ with marginals $\mu_1, \mu_2$ and support
  contained in $R$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Strong probabilistic simulation]
  \label{def:strongsim}
  \lean{PLTS.StrongProbabilisticSimulation}\leanok
  \uses{def:system, def:pmfrel}
  $R : State_C \to State_A \to \mathrm{Prop}$ is a \emph{strong} simulation of
  $sys_C$ by $sys_A$ when $R\ sys_C.init\ sys_A.init$, and every concrete step
  $s_C \xrightarrow{l} \mu_C$ from an $R$-related pair $(s_C, s_A)$ is matched by a
  single strong abstract step $s_A \xrightarrow{l} \mu_A$ (same label, no
  stuttering) with $\operatorname{PMFRel} R\ \mu_C\ \mu_A$.
\end{definition}

% Paper-level definition; not a standalone Lean declaration (see the NOTE
% below: the Lean development phrases weak simulation via
% ProbabilisticForwardSimulation directly).
A \emph{weak} probabilistic simulation is the same notion with stuttering
allowed on the abstract side: a concrete step is matched from $\delta_{s_A}$ by
a $weakTau$ (internal $l$) or a $weakStep$ with the same label (external $l$),
again with $\operatorname{PMFRel} R\ \mu_C\ \mu_A$.

\begin{definition}[Probabilistic forward simulation]
  \label{def:forwardsim}
  \lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticForwardSimulation}\leanok
  \uses{def:weaktau, def:weakstep, def:pmfrel}
  Probabilistic forward simulation is due to Segala~\cite{Segala95}.
  Here $R : State_C \to \operatorname{PMF} State_A \to \mathrm{Prop}$ relates each
  concrete state to a \emph{distribution} over abstract states. The initial
  concrete state is related to some distribution supported on $sys_A.init$, and
  from an $R$-related pair $(s_C, \mu_A)$ every concrete step
  $s_C \xrightarrow{l} \mu_C$ is matched by a weak transition
  $\mu_A \Rightarrow (\omega \mathbin{\gg\!=}\mathrm{id})$
  ($weakTau$ for internal $l$, $weakStep$ for external $l$), where
  $\omega : \operatorname{PMF}(\operatorname{PMF} State_A)$ couples $\mu_C$ with
  $R$-related abstract distributions, i.e. $\operatorname{PMFRel} R\ \mu_C\ \omega$.
\end{definition}

Forward simulation generalises weak simulation: it lets the abstract state be
tracked only up to a \emph{belief} distribution, which is precisely what the state
space $\operatorname{PMF} State_A$ of $\mathcal{D}(sys_A^w)$ records.

\section{Building blocks: functional simulation and the constructions}

The soundness proof rests on one reusable engine and three preservation results.

\begin{lemma}[Functional simulation is trace-sound]
  \label{lem:map}
  \lean{PLTS.achievableTraceDists_map}\leanok
  \uses{def:achievable}
  Let $f : X \to Y$ be a label-preserving \emph{functional} simulation between
  systems: $f(sys_X.init) = sys_Y.init$ and every step $s \xrightarrow{l} \mu$
  lifts to $f(s) \xrightarrow{l} f_\ast\mu$ (the pushforward $\mu.map\ f$). Then
  $\operatorname{ATD}(sys_X) \subseteq \operatorname{ATD}(sys_Y)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  Fix a probabilistic execution $pe_X$ of $sys_X$ with initial distribution
  $\delta_{sys_X.init}$ realising $D$. Since $f$ preserves labels, pushing each
  $X$-history $E_X$ forward to the $Y$-history $f_\ast E_X$ (apply $f$ to every
  state, leave labels alone) preserves the label sequence, hence the trace and the
  tightness status. What must be produced is a \emph{single} $sys_Y$-scheduler
  realising this pushforward, and it is recovered by \emph{disintegration}. To a
  finite $Y$-history $E_Y$ attach the fibre weight
  \[
    g(E_Y, E_X) \;=\; pe_X.probOf(E_X)
    \qquad\text{for } E_X \text{ in the fibre } f_\ast E_X = E_Y,
  \]
  and let the $Y$-scheduler at $E_Y$ emit each next move with probability
  proportional to the total $g$-weight of the fibre members emitting a preimage of
  that move. This is exactly the posterior over the $f$-fibre of $E_Y$ weighted by
  path mass --- it averages $pe_X$'s next-step law over all $X$-histories that $f$
  collapses onto $E_Y$. Because labels are untouched, the induced $sys_Y$-execution
  assigns each tight $Y$-history the summed $probOf$-mass of its fibre, so
  $traceProb$ is preserved cone by cone and the realised trace distribution is
  again $D$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Soundness of strong simulation]
  \label{thm:strongsound}
  \lean{PLTS.StrongProbabilisticSimulation.achievableTraceDists_subset}\leanok
  \uses{def:strongsim, lem:map}
  If $R$ is a strong simulation of $sys_C$ by $sys_A$, then
  $\operatorname{ATD}(sys_C) \subseteq \operatorname{ATD}(sys_A)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:strongsim, lem:map}
  A strong simulation is \emph{lockstep} --- each concrete step is matched by an
  abstract step with the same label, no stuttering --- so the proof factors through
  the \emph{coupled product} $\operatorname{simProd} sys_C\ sys_A\ R$
  (\lean{PLTS.simProd}) over $State_C \times State_A$. Its step relation
  $(s_C, s_A) \xrightarrow{l} \omega$ requires that the two marginals
  $\omega.map\ \pi_1$ and $\omega.map\ \pi_2$ be valid $sys_C$- and $sys_A$-steps
  and that $\operatorname{supp}\omega \subseteq R$; its initial state
  $(sys_C.init, sys_A.init)$ is $R$-related by the simulation's $init$ clause. Two
  inclusions compose.

  \emph{Lift} (\lean{PLTS.simProd_achievableTraceDists_superset}):
  $\operatorname{ATD}(sys_C) \subseteq \operatorname{ATD}(\operatorname{simProd}\,sys_C\,sys_A\,R)$.
  From a $sys_C$-execution $pe_C$ build the joint execution
  \lean{PLTS.simJointExec}: drive $pe_C$ on the concrete component, and whenever it
  emits $(l, \mu_C)$ from a joint history whose current pair $(s_C, s_A)$ is
  $R$-related, apply $sim.step$ to $s_C \xrightarrow{l} \mu_C$ to obtain a coupling
  $\omega$ with $\omega.map\ \pi_1 = \mu_C$, a valid abstract step
  $\omega.map\ \pi_2$, and $\operatorname{supp}\omega \subseteq R$; emit
  $(l, \omega)$. By construction the concrete projection of the joint execution is
  exactly $pe_C$ and labels are shared, so their trace distributions agree. This
  direction is purely forward (no marginalisation).

  \emph{Project}: the second projection $\pi_2$ is a functional, label-preserving
  simulation of $\operatorname{simProd} sys_C\ sys_A\ R$ by $sys_A$ --- it sends the
  joint init to $sys_A.init$, and the marginal condition makes every joint step map
  to a genuine $sys_A$-step $\pi_2(p) \xrightarrow{l} \omega.map\ \pi_2$ --- so
  Lemma~\ref{lem:map} gives
  $\operatorname{ATD}(\operatorname{simProd}\,sys_C\,sys_A\,R) \subseteq \operatorname{ATD}(sys_A)$.
  Compose the two inclusions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The weak closure preserves trace distributions]
  \label{thm:weakclosure-eq}
  \lean{PLTS.weakClosure_traceProb_eq}\leanok
  \uses{def:achievable, def:weakclosure}
  $\operatorname{ATD}(sys) = \operatorname{ATD}(sys^w)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{lem:map}
  The easy inclusion $\operatorname{ATD}(sys) \subseteq \operatorname{ATD}(sys^w)$
  (\lean{PLTS.weakClosure_traceProb_subset}) is Lemma~\ref{lem:map} with $f = id$:
  every strong $sys$-step is, in particular, a (degenerate) weak step of $sys^w$.

  The hard inclusion (\lean{PLTS.weakClosure_traceProb_superset}) turns an abstract
  scheduler $ws$ on $sys^w$ into a concrete scheduler on $sys$ with the same trace
  distribution. Algorithm~\ref{alg:expand} is the unfolding: it maintains an
  abstract execution $we$ on $sys^w$ and a concrete execution $e$ on $sys$; each
  outer iteration draws a weak transition $wt = (l, \mu)$ from $ws(we)$, takes a
  \emph{witness} scheduler realising it (\lean{PLTS.Realises}: run from
  $\delta_{last(e)}$ it halts almost surely, its halting end-state distribution is
  exactly $\mu$, and every halting trajectory has external trace $[l]$, or $[]$ when
  $l = \tau$), runs that witness to produce the concrete segment
  $e' = \tau^\ast\!\cdot l\cdot\tau^\ast$, and appends it. The control flow is
  deterministic but the draws are random, so each reachable \emph{configuration}
  $c$ is reached with a well-defined probability $reachProb(c)$
  (\lean{PLTS.reachProb}); the induced memoryless concrete scheduler is
  $expandSched\ ws$ (\lean{PLTS.expandSched}), whose next-move law at a concrete
  history is the Bayesian posterior over the next inner draw given that history.

  The fidelity equality \lean{PLTS.traceProb_expandSched_eq} is proved
  configuration by configuration (for $\tau \ne []$; $\tau = []$ is $1 = 1$). Each
  side reduces to a sum of $reachProb$:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item the abstract $traceProb$ collects the \emph{entry} configurations
      $\{\,c \mid c.e'.trans = nil,\ IsTight(c.we),\ trace(c.we) = \tau\,\}$
      (a fresh weak step has just committed);
    \item the concrete $traceProb$ collects the \emph{arrival} configurations
      $\{\,c \mid c.e'.trans \ne nil,\ IsTight(c.e),\ trace(c.e) = \tau\,\}$
      (the concrete run has just emitted the external $l$).
  \end{itemize}
  These are the \emph{same} external weak step measured one moment apart: between an
  arrival and its matching entry lies only the post-$l$ $\tau$-run and the outer
  commit, which change $last(e)$ but not the external trace. Their $reachProb$
  masses are \emph{equal} --- not merely $\le$ --- because the witness's post-$\tau$
  run halts almost surely (the $\sum haltMass = 1$ clause of $Realises$), so no mass
  leaks to divergence inside a witness run and $reachProb$ is conserved across the
  post-$\tau$ sojourn. Hence the entry- and arrival-sums agree.
\end{proof}

\begin{algorithm}
    \caption{Simulating a scheduler on $sys^w$}
    \label{alg:expand}
    \begin{algorithmic}
        \State \textbf{Input:} a PLTS $sys$ and a scheduler $ws$ on $sys^w$
        \medskip
        \State $we, e \gets sys^w.init, sys.init$
        \State $wt \gets ws(we)$
        \State \textbf{while} $wt \ne \bot$:
        \State\quad $s \gets wt.choose$ \Comment{get the scheduler inducing $wt$}
        \State\quad $e' \gets last(e)$
        \State\quad $t \gets s(e')$
        \State\quad \textbf{while} $t \ne \bot$:
        \State\quad\quad $e' \gets e'.lbl(t).out(t)$
        \State\quad\quad $t \gets s(e')$
        \State\quad $e \gets e^\frown e'$
        \State\quad $we \gets we.lbl(wt).last(e)$
        \State\quad $wt \gets ws(we)$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[The distribution-monad lift preserves trace distributions]
  \label{thm:dist-eq}
  \lean{PLTS.dist_traceProb_eq}\leanok
  \uses{def:achievable, def:dist}
  $\operatorname{ATD}(sys) = \operatorname{ATD}(\mathcal{D}(sys))$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{lem:map}
  The easy inclusion
  $\operatorname{ATD}(sys) \subseteq \operatorname{ATD}(\mathcal{D}(sys))$
  (\lean{PLTS.dist_traceProb_subset}) is Lemma~\ref{lem:map} for the Dirac
  embedding $s \mapsto \delta_s$: a step $s \xrightarrow{l} \mu$ lifts to the
  hyper-step $\delta_s \xrightarrow{l} \delta_\mu$, whose flattening is $\mu$.

  The hard inclusion (\lean{PLTS.dist_traceProb_superset}) flattens the genuine
  $\operatorname{PMF}(\operatorname{PMF} State)$-randomness of a
  $\mathcal{D}(sys)$-execution $pe'$ back to an ordinary $sys$-execution. Its
  initial distribution is $pe'.initState \mathbin{\gg\!=}\mathrm{id}$ and its
  scheduler is the \emph{lowering} \lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticExecution.lower}: at a
  concrete history $e$ with label list $labs$ and current state $s$, it forms the
  \emph{trace-cone belief} \lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticExecution.beliefTC} --- the
  posterior over those $\mathcal{D}(sys)$-histories $E$ whose full label list is
  $labs$, weighted by the mass that $E$'s current distribution places on $s$ --- and
  emits the corresponding average of $pe'$'s hyper-steps, each split into per-state
  $sys$-steps by \lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticExecution.distHyperKernel}. Conditioning on
  the \emph{full} label list (internal labels included, not just the external trace)
  keeps the belief normalizer finite --- it is a level sum --- and makes the step a
  genuine posterior. The resulting $sys$-execution has the same trace distribution as
  $pe'$. The Dirac-init clause of $\operatorname{ATD}$ is exactly what makes this
  reconstruction sound: a mixture initial distribution over
  $\operatorname{PMF} State$ need not be realised by any single $sys$-execution, and
  the belief reconstruction would then be unsound.
\end{proof}

\section{Soundness of probabilistic forward simulation}

The engine of the reduction is that forward simulation is not a new proof
obligation at all: it is \emph{definitionally} a strong simulation into
$\mathcal{D}(sys_A^w)$. The one non-formal ingredient is the transition-level
correspondence below.

\subsection{The transition-level correspondence}

A step of $\mathcal{D}(sys_A^w)$ from a distribution $\mu$ is a hyper-step of
$sys_A^w$, and it must be identified with a single weak transition of $sys_A$ from
$\mu$. That identification rests on three families of ``workhorse'' lemmas about
how weak transitions interact with mixing (the concrete/abstract subscripts are
dropped; $sys$ is an arbitrary system).

\begin{lemma}[Source decomposition]
  \label{lem:decomp}
  \lean{PLTS.weakTau_exists_pointwise, PLTS.weakStep_exists_pointwise}\leanok
  \uses{def:weaktau, def:weakstep}
  A weak transition out of a distribution factors pointwise: if
  $weakTau\ sys\ \mu\ \nu$ then there is
  $\rho : State \to \operatorname{PMF} State$ with
  $\nu = \mu \mathbin{\gg\!=} \rho$ and $weakTau\ sys\ \delta_s\ (\rho\,s)$ for every
  $s \in \operatorname{supp}\mu$; likewise for $weakStep$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  Dispatch the single witness scheduler on the (observable) start state: run from
  $\delta_s$ it defines $\rho\,s$, and averaging over $\mu$ recovers $\nu$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Source mixing]
  \label{lem:mix}
  \lean{PLTS.weakTau_of_pointwise, PLTS.weakStep_of_pointwise}\leanok
  \uses{def:weaktau, def:weakstep}
  The converse recombination: given per-point weak transitions
  $weakTau\ sys\ \delta_s\ (\rho\,s)$ for $s \in \operatorname{supp}\mu$, a single
  scheduler dispatching on the start state witnesses
  $weakTau\ sys\ \mu\ (\mu \mathbin{\gg\!=} \rho)$; likewise for $weakStep$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Target convexity]
  \label{lem:bind}
  \lean{PLTS.weakTau_bind, PLTS.weakStep_bind}\leanok
  \uses{def:weaktau, def:weakstep}
  A \emph{mixture of targets from a single point} is again a weak transition: if
  $q : \operatorname{PMF}(\operatorname{PMF} State)$ satisfies
  $weakTau\ sys\ \delta_s\ \sigma$ for every $\sigma \in \operatorname{supp} q$,
  then $weakTau\ sys\ \delta_s\ (q \mathbin{\gg\!=} \mathrm{id})$; likewise for
  $weakStep$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  The crux. Use a posterior-weighted mixing scheduler: from $\delta_s$ first draw a
  target $\sigma \sim q$ and then run $\sigma$'s witness scheduler. Almost-sure
  halting of each witness gives almost-sure halting of the mixture, and its halting
  end-state distribution is $q \mathbin{\gg\!=} \mathrm{id}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Hyper-step / weak-transition bridge]
  \label{lem:bridge}
  \lean{PLTS.hyperStep_weakClosure_of_weakTau, PLTS.weakTau_of_hyperStep_weakClosure, PLTS.hyperStep_weakClosure_of_weakStep, PLTS.weakStep_of_hyperStep_weakClosure}\leanok
  \uses{def:hyperstep, def:weakclosure, lem:decomp, lem:mix, lem:bind}
  For a distribution $\mu$ over states,
  \[
    hyperStep\ sys^w\ \mu\ l\ \nu
    \iff
    \begin{cases}
      weakTau\ sys\ \mu\ \nu & (l = \tau),\\
      weakStep\ sys\ \mu\ l\ \nu & (l \ne \tau).
    \end{cases}
  \]
  Equivalently: a step of $\mathcal{D}(sys^w)$ out of $\mu$ is exactly a weak
  transition of $sys$ out of $\mu$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{lem:decomp, lem:mix, lem:bind}
  ($\Leftarrow$) Decompose the weak transition out of $\mu$ into per-point weak
  transitions (Lemma~\ref{lem:decomp}); each is precisely a step of $sys^w$ out of a
  Dirac, so their $\mu$-average is a hyper-step $hyperStep\ sys^w\ \mu\ l\ \nu$.
  ($\Rightarrow$) A hyper-step provides, for each $s \in \operatorname{supp}\mu$, a
  distribution $p\,s$ over $sys^w$-successors of $s$ --- i.e.\ a \emph{mixture of
  weak transitions out of $\delta_s$}. Collapse that mixture to a single per-point
  weak transition with Lemma~\ref{lem:bind}, then recombine over the source with
  Lemma~\ref{lem:mix}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The reduction}

\begin{theorem}[Forward simulation $=$ strong simulation into $\mathcal{D}(\cdot^w)$]
  \label{thm:forward-equiv}
  \lean{PLTS.probabilisticForwardSimulation_iff_strong_dist_weakClosure}\leanok
  \uses{def:forwardsim, def:strongsim, def:dist, def:weakclosure, lem:bridge}
  For any relation $R : State_C \to \operatorname{PMF} State_A \to \mathrm{Prop}$,
  \[
    \text{$R$ is a forward simulation of $sys_C$ by $sys_A$}
    \iff
    \text{$R$ is a strong simulation of $sys_C$ by $\mathcal{D}(sys_A^w)$.}
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{lem:bridge}
  Both notions carry the \emph{same} relation $R$: the state type of
  $\mathcal{D}(sys_A^w)$ is $\operatorname{PMF} State_A$, exactly the type of the
  forward simulation's abstract argument. On initial states, the forward
  simulation's ``distribution supported on $sys_A.init$'' is forced to be the Dirac
  $\delta_{sys_A.init}$, which is the initial state of $\mathcal{D}(sys_A^w)$; and
  the internal vs.\ external case split coincides because $\tau$ is canonical. The
  step clauses then match verbatim under the bridge (Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge}): a
  forward step matches $s_C \xrightarrow{l} \mu_C$ by a weak transition
  $\mu_A \Rightarrow (\omega \mathbin{\gg\!=} \mathrm{id})$, while a
  $\mathcal{D}(sys_A^w)$ strong step matches it by a hyper-step
  $\mu_A \xrightarrow{l} \omega$, i.e.\
  $hyperStep\ sys_A^w\ \mu_A\ l\ (\omega \mathbin{\gg\!=}\mathrm{id})$ --- and these
  are equivalent, with the \emph{same} coupling $\omega$ and the same
  $\operatorname{PMFRel} R\ \mu_C\ \omega$ witnessing both.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Soundness of probabilistic forward simulation]
  \label{thm:forward-sound}
  \lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticForwardSimulation.achievableTraceDists_subset}\leanok
  \uses{def:forwardsim, def:achievable, thm:forward-equiv, thm:strongsound,
        thm:dist-eq, thm:weakclosure-eq}
  If $sys_C$ is forward-simulated by $sys_A$ (along some
  $R : State_C \to \operatorname{PMF} State_A \to \mathrm{Prop}$), then
  \[
    \operatorname{ATD}(sys_C) \subseteq \operatorname{ATD}(sys_A).
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{thm:forward-equiv, thm:strongsound, thm:dist-eq, thm:weakclosure-eq}
  Chain the four building blocks. By Theorem~\ref{thm:forward-equiv} the forward
  simulation $R$ is a strong simulation of $sys_C$ by $\mathcal{D}(sys_A^w)$, so
  Theorem~\ref{thm:strongsound} gives
  \[
    \operatorname{ATD}(sys_C) \subseteq \operatorname{ATD}(\mathcal{D}(sys_A^w)).
  \]
  Theorem~\ref{thm:dist-eq}, applied to the system $sys_A^w$, gives
  $\operatorname{ATD}(\mathcal{D}(sys_A^w)) = \operatorname{ATD}(sys_A^w)$, and
  Theorem~\ref{thm:weakclosure-eq}, applied to $sys_A$, gives
  $\operatorname{ATD}(sys_A^w) = \operatorname{ATD}(sys_A)$. Composing,
  \[
    \operatorname{ATD}(sys_C)
      \subseteq \operatorname{ATD}(\mathcal{D}(sys_A^w))
      = \operatorname{ATD}(sys_A^w)
      = \operatorname{ATD}(sys_A). \qedhere
  \]
\end{proof}

The two transformations do all the work: $\cdot^w$ turns the stuttering allowed by
a weak/forward simulation into ordinary strong steps, and $\mathcal{D}(\cdot)$
turns the ``distribution over abstract states'' bookkeeping of a forward
simulation into ordinary states --- and, crucially,
Theorems~\ref{thm:weakclosure-eq} and~\ref{thm:dist-eq} guarantee that neither
transformation changes the observable trace distributions. The analogous soundness
statement for \emph{weak} simulation
% NOTE: PLTS.WeakProbabilisticSimulation is described in SimDefs.lean docstrings but
% not (yet) declared in the code; the \lean{} cross-link is demoted to \texttt{} so
% checkdecls stays green. Restore the \lean{} macro when the declaration lands.
(\texttt{PLTS.WeakProbabilisticSimulation.achievableTraceDists\_subset}) is the same
argument using only $\cdot^w$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:weakclosure-eq}), since a weak
simulation is a strong simulation into $sys_A^w$.

\subsection{Consequences and constructions}

Safety properties are stated on the supports of achievable trace distributions.
The bridge below is what carries them from a system to every system it simulates.

\begin{theorem}[Trace-support bridge]
  \label{thm:trace-support}
  \lean{PLTS.exists_exec_of_traceProb_ne_zero}\lean{PLTS.is_exec_of_probOf_ne_zero}\lean{PLTS.safety_transfer}\leanok
  A trace with positive trace probability under a Dirac-initialised
  probabilistic execution is the trace of a genuine execution of the system,
  with trace membership characterised by the execution's events; safety of
  trace supports transfers along inclusion of achievable trace distributions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:probexec, def:traceprob, def:exec, def:achievable}
  Fix a Dirac-initialised probabilistic execution and a trace $t$ with positive trace probability.
  Positive trace probability forces a positive-mass path, and reading its labels off that path yields a genuine execution of the system whose trace is $t$; trace membership is then characterised by that execution's events.
  For the transfer, suppose $\operatorname{ATD}(sys_C) \subseteq \operatorname{ATD}(sys_A)$ and that every trace in the support of every achievable trace distribution of $sys_A$ is safe.
  Each achievable trace distribution of $sys_C$ is one of $sys_A$, so its support is safe as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The process-algebra operators]
  \label{def:process-algebra}
  \lean{PLTS.System.parallel}\lean{PLTS.System.interleave}\lean{PLTS.System.abstract}\leanok
  \uses{def:system}
  Three operators build compound systems over a shared label type.

  \emph{Parallel composition} $sys_1 \parallel sys_2$ has state space
  $State_1 \times State_2$ and initial state the pair of initial states. It
  synchronises on visible labels and interleaves on $\tau$: a step labelled
  $l \neq \tau$ requires both components to step on $l$, with successor the
  independent product $\mu_1 \otimes \mu_2$; a step labelled $\tau$ moves
  exactly one component and holds the other fixed.

  \emph{Interleaving} $\operatorname{interleave} sys$ of an indexed family has
  state space $\prod_i State_i$ and moves exactly one component per step, at
  any label: $s \xrightarrow{l} \mu$ when some $sys_i$ has
  $s_i \xrightarrow{l} \mu_i$ and $\mu$ is the independent product of $\mu_i$
  with the Diracs on the unchanged components.

  \emph{Abstraction} $sys.\operatorname{abstract} L$ leaves states and initial
  state untouched and relabels the alphabet: every $L$-labelled transition
  becomes a $\tau$-transition, and transitions on labels outside $L$ are kept
  as they are.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Extended alphabets and restriction]
  \label{def:relabel}
  \lean{PLTS.instSilentSum}\lean{PLTS.System.relabel}\lean{PLTS.System.relabel_isLTS}\lean{PLTS.System.abstract_isLTS}\leanok
  A composition whose components rendezvous on labels the composite is not meant to expose is built over the extended alphabet $\mathrm{Label} \oplus \mathrm{Extra}$, with $\mathrm{Extra}$ carrying the auxiliary labels.
  The silent label of the extended alphabet is $\mathrm{inl}\,\tau$, so every auxiliary label $\mathrm{inr}\,e$ is observable and hence hideable by $\mathrm{abstract}$.
  Restriction along the left embedding, $\mathrm{relabel}(sys)$, keeps the state space and the initial state of $sys$ and retains exactly its $\mathrm{inl}$-labelled transitions.
  Auxiliary transitions are discarded, so they must be abstracted to $\tau$ first if they are to survive.
  Restriction discards transitions and not states, so it preserves the LTS property; abstraction preserves it as well, relabelling each transition and keeping its distribution.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Idle padding and instance families]
  \label{def:idle-family}
  \lean{PLTS.System.withIdle}\lean{PLTS.System.family}\leanok
  \uses{def:process-algebra}
  Idle self-loops on foreign labels make the full synchronisation of
  Definition~\ref{def:process-algebra} emulate sync-set composition.
  The $\mathbb{N}$-indexed instance family moves one instance per owned or
  silent label, broadcasts corruption to all instances, and idles otherwise.
  Families of LTS instances are LTS.\lean{PLTS.System.family_isLTS}\leanok
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The full-synchronisation product]
  \label{def:syncproduct}
  \lean{PLTS.System.syncProduct}\lean{PLTS.System.syncProduct_isLTS}\leanok
  \uses{def:idle-family}
  Let $sys_i : \mathrm{System}\,(State_i)\,\mathrm{Label}$ for $i$ ranging over a finite index type.
  On a visible label $l \neq \tau$ the product $\mathrm{syncProduct}(sys)$ steps every component on $l$ simultaneously, and the joint successor is the independent product of the per-component distributions.
  On $\tau$ exactly one component steps and the others hold their state.
  Ownership of a label is carried by the components and not by the operator, which stays a single conjunction over the family.
  A component idles on the labels it does not own (Definition~\ref{def:idle-family}), so a label owned by exactly two components moves those two and leaves every other component in place.
  A label no component offers is blocked, the conjunction over the family having no witness.
  A product of LTS components is an LTS: a product of Diracs is the Dirac on the tuple of their points, and a single-coordinate update of an all-Dirac family is again a Dirac.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Functional strong matchings]
  \label{def:strongfunctional}
  \lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticForwardSimulation.ofStrongFunctional}\lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticForwardSimulation.congr_rel}\lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticForwardSimulation.ofStrongFunctional_diracRel}\lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticForwardSimulation.ofStrongFunctional_converse}\lean{PLTS.prodPMF_map_swap}\lean{PLTS.System.parallel_swap_step}\leanok
  \uses{def:forwardsim, def:process-algebra}
  Let $f : State_C \to State_A$ carry $sys_C.init$ to $sys_A.init$ and carry every transition $s \xrightarrow{l} \mu$ of $sys_C$ to the transition $f\,s \xrightarrow{l} \mu.\mathrm{map}\,f$ of $sys_A$ on the same label.
  Then the graph of $f$, the relation $R\,s\,\nu \iff \nu = \delta_{f\,s}$, is a probabilistic forward simulation of $sys_C$ by $sys_A$.
  Let $g : State_A \to State_C$ carry $sys_A.init$ to $sys_C.init$, and let every transition $g\,q \xrightarrow{l} \mu$ of $sys_C$ be $\mu = \nu.\mathrm{map}\,g$ for some transition $q \xrightarrow{l} \nu$ of $sys_A$.
  Then the converse of the graph of $g$, the relation $R\,p\,\nu \iff \exists q,\ \nu = \delta_q \wedge p = g\,q$, is a probabilistic forward simulation of $sys_C$ by $sys_A$.
  Neither system need be an LTS, and $g$ need be neither injective nor surjective; states outside its image are simply unrelated.
  Either matching is restated along the Dirac lift $\operatorname{diracRel}$ by transport along a pointwise equivalence of relations, which is the shape the composed relations of the process algebra are built from.
  The coordinate exchange $\mathrm{swap}(a,b) = (b,a)$ is such a matching between $sys_1 \parallel sys_2$ and $sys_2 \parallel sys_1$ in both directions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:forwardsim, def:process-algebra}
  Take the graph of $f$ first.
  The initial clause is the hypothesis $f(sys_C.init) = sys_A.init$, read as $\delta_{f(sys_C.init)}$ supported on $sys_A.init$.
  For the step clause, the pushforward $\mu.\mathrm{map}\,f$ matches $\mu$ outcome by outcome, so the coupling is the pushforward of $\mu$ along $s \mapsto (s, \delta_{f\,s})$, and the abstract side answers with its single strong step, read as a weak transition.
  Now take the converse of the graph of $g$.
  A related pair is $(g\,q, \delta_q)$, and the hypothesis reflects the concrete step $g\,q \xrightarrow{l} \mu$ to an abstract step $q \xrightarrow{l} \nu$ with $\mu = \nu.\mathrm{map}\,g$.
  The coupling is the pushforward of $\nu$ along $q' \mapsto (g\,q', \delta_{q'})$, whose first marginal is $\mu$ and whose support lies in the relation.
  Transport along a pointwise equivalence of relations restates either matching along $\operatorname{diracRel}$.
  For $\mathrm{swap}$ the step clause is that a product distribution pushed forward along $\mathrm{swap}$ is the product taken the other way round, which is exactly the step relation of $sys_2 \parallel sys_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Transitivity of probabilistic forward simulation]
  \label{thm:forward-trans}
  \lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticForwardSimulation.trans}\leanok
  \uses{def:forwardsim}
  If $sys_C$ is forward-simulated by $sys_B$ along $R_{BC}$ and $sys_B$ is
  forward-simulated by $sys_A$ along $R_{AB}$, then $sys_C$ is
  forward-simulated by $sys_A$ along the composite relation
  $\operatorname{compRel} R_{BC}\ R_{AB}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:forwardsim, def:weakstep, lem:bridge}
  The composite relation holds of $s_C$ and $\mu_A$ when some intermediate
  $\mu_B$ satisfies $R_{BC}$ against $s_C$ and simulates $\mu_A$ under
  $R_{AB}$. For the initial condition, bind the intermediate initial
  distribution against the constant abstract one. For the step condition,
  match the concrete step on the $sys_B$ side, which yields a weak transition
  out of $\mu_B$, and then lift that weak transition along $R_{AB}$ to a weak
  transition of $sys_A$. That lift is the crux: it promotes a simulation of
  single steps to a simulation of the $\tau^\ast \cdot l \cdot \tau^\ast$
  segments a weak transition is built from, and it supplies the coupling
  witnessing the composite relation on the successors.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Precongruence for parallel composition]
  \label{thm:precong-parallel}
  \lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticForwardSimulation.parallel_right}\leanok
  \uses{def:forwardsim, def:process-algebra}
  If $sys_C$ is forward-simulated by $sys_A$ along $R$, then for every context
  $sys_B$ the composition $sys_C \parallel sys_B$ is forward-simulated by
  $sys_A \parallel sys_B$, along the pointwise lifted relation
  $\operatorname{parallelRel} R$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:process-algebra, lem:decomp, lem:mix, lem:bind}
  Case on the three shapes of a composite step. A synchronised visible step is
  answered by the abstract component's weak transition on the same label,
  tensored with the context's own step on that label; a $\tau$-step of the
  simulated component is answered by its weak transition, tensored with the
  context held fixed; a $\tau$-step of the context is answered by the context
  alone, the simulated side stuttering. Each case tensors a weak transition
  with a fixed or matching factor, which is where the source-decomposition,
  source-mixing and target-convexity lemmas are used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Precongruence for interleaving]
  \label{thm:precong-interleave}
  \lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticForwardSimulation.interleave}\leanok
  \uses{def:forwardsim, def:process-algebra}
  If $sysA_i$ forward-simulates $sysC_i$ along $R_i$ for every index $i$, then
  $\operatorname{interleave} sysA$ forward-simulates
  $\operatorname{interleave} sysC$, along $\operatorname{interleaveRel} R$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:process-algebra, lem:decomp, lem:mix}
  A step of the interleaving moves exactly one component $i$. Answer it by the
  weak transition $R_i$ supplies for that component and leave every other
  component stuttering; the product coupling of the componentwise couplings
  witnesses the lifted relation on the successors. The source-decomposition and
  source-mixing lemmas supply the transition on a product of distributions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Precongruence for abstraction]
  \label{thm:precong-abstract}
  \lean{PLTS.ProbabilisticForwardSimulation.abstract}\leanok
  \uses{def:forwardsim, def:process-algebra}
  If $sys_C$ is forward-simulated by $sys_A$ along $R$, then
  $sys_C.\operatorname{abstract} L$ is forward-simulated by
  $sys_A.\operatorname{abstract} L$ along the same relation $R$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:process-algebra, def:weakstep}
  The initial condition is unchanged, since abstraction does not touch initial
  states. For the step condition, split on whether the concrete transition
  carries a hidden label. If it does, the abstracted label is $\tau$, and the
  abstract weak transition on the hidden label becomes an abstract weak
  transition on $\tau$, since hiding maps every one of its segments to $\tau$.
  If it does not, the label survives abstraction on both sides and the matching
  weak transition transports segment by segment. Either way the coupling is the
  one $R$ already provides.
\end{proof}

\section{Case study: Asynchronous Byzantine Agreement}

This section is a case study: the verification of an implementation of
Asynchronous Byzantine Agreement against a small specification, by
probabilistic forward simulation, built on the five results proved above.
The setting is $n$ processes, each holding an input bit, of which at most $f$
are Byzantine, with $3f < n$, communicating by asynchronous message passing.
The development lives in the opt-in \texttt{Leslie2Protocols} Lake library.

\subsection{Setting and conventions}

Three protocols occur in the case study.
Each is given as a list of properties over the calls and returns of its
interface.
A property speaks only of \emph{correct} processes, that is, of processes
that are never corrupted.

Asynchronous Byzantine Agreement (ABA).
Processes start with a bit in $\{0,1\}$ and may return a bit.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Validity: if a correct process returns $b$ then a correct process had
    $b$ as input.
  \item Agreement: if two correct processes return, then their outputs are
    equal.
  \item Almost-sure Termination: if $n-f$ correct processes take part in the
    protocol then, with probability $1$, all correct processes will eventually
    return.
\end{itemize}

Graded Binding Crusader Agreement (GBCA).
Processes start with a binary input in $\{0,1\}$ and may return a value in
\[
  \{(0,A),\ (0,B),\ (\bot,C),\ (1,B),\ (1,A)\}.
\]
\begin{itemize}
  \item Validity: if a correct process does not return $(b,A)$ then a correct
    process had $1-b$ as input.
  \item Graded Agreement: if two correct processes return $(b,X)$ and
    $(b',X')$ then $b = 0 \Rightarrow b' \neq 1$ and
    $X = A \Rightarrow X' \neq \bot$.
  \item Binding: when a correct process returns then a bound value $b$ is
    defined and, in all extensions of the execution, correct processes do not
    return $(1-b,B)$ or $(1-b,A)$.
  \item Termination: if $n-f$ correct processes take part in the protocol then
    all correct processes will eventually return.
\end{itemize}
In the second clause of Graded Agreement the symbol $\bot$ stands in the grade
component, where the return domain admits only $A$, $B$ and $C$.
It is read here as the grade $C$, the sole grade paired with the value $\bot$.
The encoding realises that reading: an $A$-return and a $C$-return of the same
instance exclude one another.

Weak Common Coin (WCC).
Processes start with no input and may return a bit.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\varepsilon$-Goodness: with probability $\varepsilon$, all correct
    processes return $0$ (resp.\ $1$).
  \item Unpredictability: until $f+1$ processes have called WCC, all outcomes
    are still possible.
  \item $(1-\failmass)$-sure Termination: if $n-f$ correct processes take part in
    the protocol then, with probability $1-\failmass$, all correct processes will
    eventually return.
\end{itemize}

What this development establishes is Validity and Agreement for ABA,
machine-checked.
What it does not establish is the rest.
Termination, Unpredictability and fairness are out of scope.
The weak common coin is held at specification level rather than implemented:
its own implementation over Gather and SRSD is not modelled here, so
$\varepsilon$-Goodness enters as an assumption.

Every protocol $P$ with sub-protocols $Q$ is treated in two moves.
The first move is substitution: in a fixed context, each sub-implementation is
replaced by the corresponding specification, which is sound by precongruence.
The second move is the core simulation: the resulting hybrid, the core
algorithm of $P$ composed with the specifications of its sub-protocols, is
simulated by the specification of $P$.
Composing the two inclusions places the implementation of $P$ inside the
specification of $P$.
The recursion stops at GBCA, which is carried to implementation level.
GBCA calls no sub-protocol, so no further substitution arises there.
WCC is left at specification level.
The two implementation-level encodings, the GBCA instance and the ABA core, are
monolithic automata over the whole system state; Section~\ref{sec:aba-perproc}
shows each equal, on achievable trace distributions, to the composition of its
per-process automata.
That section then assembles both per-process layers into the hybrid
(Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-assembly}) and removes the layer boundary, presenting ABA
as $n$ programs beside a coin oracle (Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-flat}).

\paragraph{Object names.}
Transition System~1 is the ABA specification, Transition System~2 the GBCA
specification, and Transition System~3 the WCC specification.
Algorithm~1 is the ABA core algorithm and Algorithm~2 the GBCA implementation.
These names are used throughout this section and follow the numbering of the
source blueprint~\cite{LeslieBP}.

\paragraph{Deviations.}
The encodings depart from~\cite{LeslieBP} in catalogued ways.
Each departure carries a label D1--D17 and is cited at the point where it
applies.
The labels D2, D6 and D7 are unused.
\begin{itemize}
  \item D1: corruption is a total deterministic transform.
  \item D3: the re-propose guard on rule~7 of Transition System~1.
  \item D4: the WCC \texttt{guess} label and the \texttt{guess} state field are
    omitted.
  \item D5: a set-based authenticated network with per-sender message pools.
  \item D8: protocol sends gated on prior participation.
  \item D9: $0$-based round indexing.
  \item D10: the per-round bookkeeping between the coin return and the next
    call fused into the $retW$ step, which multicasts DECIDED on an $A$ grade.
  \item D11: Byzantine call- and return-driver rules at each handshake.
  \item D12$'$: per-process DECIDED sent and received pools, delivery per
    (receiver, sender, bit), and Byzantine equivocation at that layer.
  \item D13: the Validity provenance apparatus on Transition System~1, namely a
    ghost record of genuine calls, an $f+1$ support guard on the mixed-bind
    rule, a provenance guard on re-proposal, and a Byzantine fill rule.
  \item D14: $f+1$ support guards on the GBCA specification's binding, on the
    dissenting bit at its $B$-return, and on each of the two bits at its
    $C$-return.
  \item D15: the $F$-blind form of those support counts, namely $f+1$ distinct
    callers-or-corrupted.
  \item D16: the two-phase abstract twin used by the core simulation.
  \item D17: a fourth outcome $\dagger$, of mass $\failmass$, on the single
    distribution both coin resolutions follow, rule~5 of Transition System~1 and
    rule~3 of Transition System~3.
    It carries the delivery failure of the $\varepsilon$-correct secret-sharing
    coin implementation that the source builds on, where both of the source's
    resolutions range over $\{0,1,\top\}$ only.
    A $\dagger$ round of Transition System~3 enables no return.
    A $\dagger$ round of Transition System~1 is frozen: no process adopts the
    bound value and none re-proposes.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Outside the model.}
Two features of~\cite{LeslieBP} are deliberately not encoded.
The first is fairness: the source marks each transition fair or unfair, and a
fair execution is one that is finite and ends in a fair deadlock or contains
infinitely many fair transitions.
The second is partial information: the source carries adversaries given by a
pair of observation functions on states and on labels, together with the belief
construction that turns such an adversary into an omniscient one on a derived
system.
Neither appears here.
Every adversary in this section is omniscient, so the observation functions are
the identity and the belief construction is not needed.
The fairness markings bear only on liveness, which is out of scope.

\paragraph{Notation.}
Write $[n]$ for the identifier set and $F \subseteq [n]$ for the corrupted set.
The quorum predicate is
\[
  |\{ id \in [n] \setminus F : call[id] \neq \bot \} \cup F| \ \geq\ n - f .
\]
Values range over $\{0,1,\bot,\top,\dagger\}$, where $\bot$ is unresolved,
$\top$ is an adversarially resolved outcome, and $\dagger$ is a resolution that
delivers to no process (D17).
The cross-domain equality $agrees$ holds of $\bot$ with $\bot$ and of $b$ with
$b$.
Both $\top$ and $\dagger$ agree with nothing.

Every transition rule is displayed as a guard above a labelled step into an
explicit distribution.
A Dirac target and a genuinely probabilistic one are therefore distinguishable
at sight.
Exactly two transitions in the whole development are non-Dirac: the coin rule
of Transition System~1 and the coin rule of Transition System~3.
Each resolves one coin, and both follow the same distribution, displayed in
Definition~\ref{def:aba-params}.

\subsection{Parameters and the shared alphabet}

\begin{definition}[Parameters and the coin]
  \label{def:aba-params}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.Params}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Params.wccPMF}\leanok
  The parameters: $n$ processes, at most $f$ corrupted with $3f < n$, coin
  goodness $\varepsilon$, and coin failure probability $\failmass$, subject to
  $2\varepsilon + \failmass \le 1$.
  The Lean field carrying $\failmass$ is named $\delta$.
  One distribution resolves both coins of the case study, that of rule~5 of
  Transition System~1 and that of rule~3 of Transition System~3:
  \[
    \mathrm{wccPMF} \ =\ \varepsilon \cdot \delta(0) \ +\
      \varepsilon \cdot \delta(1) \ +\
      (1 - 2\varepsilon - \failmass) \cdot \delta(\top) \ +\
      \failmass \cdot \delta(\dagger).
  \]
  The constraint $2\varepsilon + \failmass \le 1$ is what keeps the mass on
  $\top$ non-negative, so that the four outcomes carry total mass one.
  The outcome $\dagger$ is the resolution that delivers to no process (D17).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Shared alphabet]
  \label{def:aba-labels}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.Lab}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Lab.hiddenAPI}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-params}
  All systems of the case study share one label type: the ABA API, the
  round-tagged GBCA/WCC APIs (hidden in the hybrids), corruption
  $\mathrm{fail}(id)$, and $\tau$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The ABA specification and its safety}

\begin{definition}[The ABA specification, repaired]
  \label{def:aba-spec}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.SpecStep}\lean{PLTS.ABA.spec}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-labels}
  Transition System 1 of~\cite{LeslieBP}, ten rules, with four deviations.
  Corruption is a total deterministic transform (D1).
  A ghost map $input$ records genuine $\mathrm{callABA}$ events: rule 1 writes it unconditionally, the input-enabledness loop rule 2 writes it first-write-wins (D13).
  The mixed-bind rule 4 carries an $f{+}1$-support guard, requiring $f{+}1$ callers of the bound bit, counted $F$-blind (D13).
  The re-propose rule 7 carries the guard $(val = \bot \wedge (input\,id = b \vee bind = b)) \vee val = b$: while undecided a process re-proposes only a recorded own input or the bound value, and once the decision value is fixed it re-proposes only $val$ (D3, D13).
  A tenth rule, $\mathrm{callByzFill}$, lets a corrupted process fill an empty call slot with an arbitrary bit and no ghost record (D13), the abstract image of the concrete adversary's hidden Byzantine $\mathrm{callG}$ drivers.
  The source blueprint's $Initial$ clause mentions an undeclared $out$ field; the field is omitted.
  The rule-7 and rule-4 guards are load-bearing: without them the specification violates Agreement and Validity respectively; the two counterexamples follow the rule listing.
  At most $f$ processes are ever corrupted, so $f{+}1$ distinct supporters always include a never-corrupted one, and $3f < n$ supplies $n - 2f \ge f + 1$.
\end{definition}

The specification has six control states: \emph{unbound} ($bind = \bot$), \emph{bound} ($bind \neq \bot$, $coin = \bot$), \emph{coin differs} ($coin$ is the other bit or $\top$), \emph{coin agrees} ($coin$ is the bound bit), \emph{coin dead} ($coin = \dagger$), and \emph{decided} ($val \neq \bot$).
Rule 5 (\texttt{coinFlip}) is the branch out of \emph{bound}, and it is the only probabilistic transition of the system.
Its outcomes carry $\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon$, $1-2\varepsilon-\failmass$ and $\failmass$, so a resolution matches the bound bit with probability $\varepsilon$ and fails to deliver with probability $\failmass$ (D17).
On a match rule 6 (\texttt{adopt}) writes the bound bit into an empty honest slot and rule 7 (\texttt{repropose}) is disabled.
On a mismatch rule 7 is enabled and rule 6 is disabled.
On $coin = \dagger$ both are disabled, rule 6 because $\dagger$ agrees with nothing and rule 7 by its $coin \neq \dagger$ conjunct.
Rule 4 (\texttt{mixed}) binds under a quorum on both bits, with an $f{+}1$-support guard on the bound bit.
Rule 3 (\texttt{unanim}) writes $val \leftarrow\,!b$ when no honest process holds $b$.
Enabledness is wider than the control graph shows.
Rule 2 fires in every state, rule 3 is also enabled on \emph{coin differs}, and rules 3 and 5 to 8 remain enabled on \emph{decided}, where rule 3 rewrites $val$ to itself.
Rule 5 is enabled again on \emph{coin dead}, where the round is still reset and bound, and a second resolution is the only transition that leaves that state.
The provenance guards on rules 4 and 7 exclude the unanimity-overwrite (D3) and later-corrupted-input (D13) traces.

\noindent\textbf{Transition System 1: the ABA specification.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{State.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $call : [n] \to \{0,1,\bot\}$, the pending round input of each process, $\bot$ when unset.
  \item $ret : [n] \to \mathbb{B}$, which processes have returned.
  \item $bind : \{0,1,\bot\}$, the bound value, the write-target of the unanimity and mixed rules.
  \item $val : \{0,1,\bot\}$, the decision value; once it is a bit, every return carries it.
  \item $coin : \{0,1,\bot,\top,\dagger\}$, the current coin outcome: $\bot$ until resolved, $\top$ the adversarial one, and $\dagger$ the failed one (D17).
  \item $F \subseteq [n]$, the corrupted set.
  \item $input : [n] \to \{0,1,\bot\}$, a ghost record of the genuine $\mathrm{callABA}$ events (D13).
\end{itemize}
\noindent Here $\mathbb{B}$ is the two-element type, written $0,1$ for protocol bits and $\mathrm{false},\mathrm{true}$ for flags.
The equation $bind = coin$ is the cross-domain equality of $\{0,1,\bot\}$ with $\{0,1,\bot,\top,\dagger\}$: either both sides are $\bot$, or both are the same bit.
The outcomes $\top$ and $\dagger$ agree with nothing.

\noindent\textbf{Labels.}
$\mathrm{callABA}(id,b)$, $\mathrm{retABA}(id,b)$, $\mathrm{fail}(id)$ and $\tau$, for $id \in [n]$ and $b \in \mathbb{B}$.

\noindent\textbf{Input-enabled labels.}
$\mathrm{callABA}(id,b)$, by the unconditional loop rule~2, and $\mathrm{fail}(id)$, whose rule~9 is total (D1).

\noindent\textbf{Initial.}
$call = \bot^{[n]}$, $ret = \mathrm{false}^{[n]}$, $bind = \bot$, $val = \bot$, $coin = \bot$, $F = \emptyset$ and $input = \bot^{[n]}$, the superscript marking a constant map on $[n]$.

\medskip
\noindent In each rule $id \in [n]$ and $b \in \mathbb{B}$ are parameters, and $s$ is the source state.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.} \texttt{callSet} (D13).
Guard $call[id] = \bot$ and $bind = \bot$.
\[ s \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{callABA}(id,b)\;}
   \delta\bigl(s[\,call[id] := b,\ input[id] := b\,]\bigr) \]

\noindent\textbf{2.} \texttt{callLoop} (D13).
No guard.
\[ s \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{callABA}(id,b)\;}
   \begin{cases}
     \delta\bigl(s[\,input[id] := b\,]\bigr) & \text{if } input[id] = \bot,\\
     \delta(s) & \text{otherwise.}
   \end{cases} \]

\noindent\textbf{3.} \texttt{unanim}.
Guard
\[ \begin{aligned}
     &\bigl|\{id \in [n]\setminus F \mid call[id] \neq \bot\} \cup F\bigr| \ge n - f,\\
     &\forall\, id \in [n]\setminus F,\ call[id] \neq b.
   \end{aligned} \]
\[ s \xrightarrow{\;\tau\;}
   \delta\bigl(s[\,call := \bot^{[n]},\ bind := 1-b,\ val := 1-b,\ coin := \bot\,]\bigr) \]

\noindent\textbf{4.} \texttt{mixed} (D13).
Guard
\[ \begin{aligned}
     &\bigl|\{id \in [n]\setminus F \mid call[id] \neq \bot\} \cup F\bigr| \ge n - f,\\
     &\exists\, id \in [n]\setminus F,\ call[id] = 1,\\
     &\exists\, id \in [n]\setminus F,\ call[id] = 0,\\
     &\bigl|\{id \in [n] \mid call[id] = b\}\bigr| \ge f + 1.
   \end{aligned} \]
\[ s \xrightarrow{\;\tau\;}
   \delta\bigl(s[\,call := \bot^{[n]},\ bind := b,\ coin := \bot\,]\bigr) \]

\noindent\textbf{5.} \texttt{coinFlip} (D17).
Guard $call = \bot^{[n]}$ and $bind \neq \bot$.
\[ \begin{aligned}
     s \xrightarrow{\;\tau\;}\ \
     &\varepsilon\cdot\delta\bigl(s[\,coin := 0\,]\bigr)
     + \varepsilon\cdot\delta\bigl(s[\,coin := 1\,]\bigr)\\
     &{}+ (1-2\varepsilon-\failmass)\cdot\delta\bigl(s[\,coin := \top\,]\bigr)
     + \failmass\cdot\delta\bigl(s[\,coin := \dagger\,]\bigr)
   \end{aligned} \]

\noindent\textbf{6.} \texttt{adopt}.
Guard $call[id] = \bot$ and $bind = coin$.
\[ s \xrightarrow{\;\tau\;} \delta\bigl(s[\,call[id] := bind\,]\bigr) \]

\noindent\textbf{7.} \texttt{repropose} (D3, D13, D17).
Guard
\[ \begin{aligned}
     &call[id] = \bot,\quad bind \neq coin,\quad coin \neq \dagger,\\
     &\bigl(val = \bot \wedge (input[id] = b \vee bind = b)\bigr) \vee val = b.
   \end{aligned} \]
\[ s \xrightarrow{\;\tau\;} \delta\bigl(s[\,call[id] := b\,]\bigr) \]
The conjunct $coin \neq \dagger$ is the freeze of D17: a resolution that delivers to no process leaves a process nothing to re-propose from.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{8.} \texttt{ret}.
Guard $val = b$ and $ret[id] = \mathrm{false}$.
\[ s \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{retABA}(id,b)\;}
   \delta\bigl(s[\,ret[id] := \mathrm{true}\,]\bigr) \]

\noindent\textbf{9.} \texttt{fail} (D1).
No guard.
\[ s \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{fail}(id)\;}
   \begin{cases}
     \delta\bigl(s[\,F := F \cup \{id\}\,]\bigr)
       & \text{if } id \notin F \text{ and } |F| < f,\\
     \delta(s) & \text{otherwise.}
   \end{cases} \]

\noindent\textbf{10.} \texttt{callByzFill} (D13).
Guard $id \in F$ and $call[id] = \bot$.
\[ s \xrightarrow{\;\tau\;} \delta\bigl(s[\,call[id] := b\,]\bigr) \]

\medskip
\noindent The guards on rules~4 and~7 are load-bearing.
Without the rule-7 guard the specification violates Agreement.
After a unanimous-$v$ round sets $val := v$ and a process returns $v$, an arbitrary re-proposal of $1-v$ lets rule~3 overwrite $val := 1-v$, and a second process returns $1-v$.
Without the rule-4 support guard the specification violates Validity.
At $n = 4$, $f = 1$ with inputs $1,0,0,0$ the value $1$ binds off its sole holder, that holder is then corrupted, and $1$ is re-proposed everywhere and decided, yet every never-corrupted process inputs $0$.

\dynamics{How a decision arises}{%
Which rule fires is scheduler nondeterminism, resolved by the adversary; the
coin is the only source of randomness.
The coin rule is enabled only on a round that is already reset and bound, so
the bound bit is committed before the coin resolves.
A coin outcome matching the bound bit has probability $\varepsilon$.
On such a match the re-propose rule is disabled, since its guard requires
disagreement, and a fresh call is disabled, since rule~1 requires the round
unbound.
Adoption, which writes the bound bit into the call slot, is then the only rule
that refills the slot of an honest process.
Corrupted slots may still be filled with either bit by the Byzantine fill rule,
but the unanimity rule's blocking condition quantifies over honest processes
only.
Once a quorum has spoken and no honest process holds the opposite bit,
unanimity is \emph{enabled}, never forced: the
scheduler may decline it forever.
No safety argument therefore uses $\varepsilon$, and the claim that the
protocol decides with probability $1$ under fair scheduling is a separate
liveness statement, not proved here.

With probability $\failmass$ the coin resolves to $\dagger$ instead, and the
round freezes.
The round is reset when the coin resolves, so every call slot is empty at that
point.
Adoption is disabled, since $\dagger$ agrees with nothing, and re-proposal is
disabled by its $coin \neq \dagger$ conjunct, so no honest slot is filled
afterwards either.
The Byzantine fill rule does not compensate: the quorum count
$|\{id \in [n]\setminus F : call[id] \neq \bot\} \cup F|$ counts a corrupted
process through $F$ whether its slot is filled or not.
That count therefore stays at $|F| \le f$, and $3f < n$ gives $f < n - f$, so
while the coin reads $\dagger$ neither the unanimity rule nor the mixed bind is
enabled, and the round produces no bind and no decision.
The freeze lifts only by a second resolution of the coin, rule~5 asking just
that the round be reset and bound, which a $\dagger$ outcome leaves true.
A single Byzantine fill closes that exit too, since rule~5 requires every call
slot to be empty.
}

\begin{theorem}[Safety of the ABA specification]
  \label{thm:aba-spec-safe}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.spec_safe}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-spec, thm:trace-support}
  Every trace in the support of every achievable trace distribution of the repaired specification satisfies Validity and Agreement.\lean{PLTS.ABA.ValidityTrace}\leanok\lean{PLTS.ABA.AgreementTrace}\leanok{}
  Validity is the paper form: every return $\mathrm{retABA}\,id\,b$ at trace position $m$ is preceded by a call $\mathrm{callABA}\,id'\,b$ at some $k < m$ whose caller $id'$ is never corrupted anywhere along the trace.
  Agreement is that any two returns carry the same bit.
  Both predicates quantify over all returns, corrupted returners included, and are on that axis stronger than the contracts' correct-process phrasing.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-spec, thm:trace-support}
  By Theorem~\ref{thm:trace-support} it suffices to prove the two predicates on the events of every genuine execution, which we do by induction over the ten rules with the invariants $\mathrm{SpecInv}$ and $\mathrm{ValInv}$.
  For Agreement, $\mathrm{SpecInv}$ makes the decision value $val$ write-once: the unanimity rule can rewrite $val$ only to itself, since a $b$-quorum and a $(1-b)$-quorum would meet under $f < n - f$, and the rule-7 (D3) guard blocks the re-proposal that would otherwise flip it.
  For Validity, $\mathrm{ValInv}$ carries provenance conjuncts tying each $bind$ and $val$ to an $f{+}1$ $F$-blind support, each member of which is either ghost-recorded as inputting the value or corrupted.
  The at most $f$ ever-corrupted processes, obtained as a trace-level fold of the $\mathrm{failSet}$, leave one of the $f{+}1$ supporters a never-corrupted recorded caller by a budget pigeonhole.
  Positionality of that caller, a call at some $k < m$, is read off the same label-history fold.
\end{proof}

\dynamics{What the grades promise}{%
The grade records how much of the round its holder is committed to.
An $A$-return commits to the bound value and is the grade that licenses a
decision.
A $B$-return carries the bound value as the next estimate but records that
dissent was seen.
A $C$-return carries no value, and its holder falls back on the coin.
The $A$- and $C$-latches are mutually exclusive: once an $A$-return has
occurred no $C$-return is enabled at that instance, and once a $C$-return has
occurred no $A$-return is.
The binding, the dissenting bit at a $B$-return and each of the two bits at a
$C$-return are gated on $f+1$ distinct supporters, counted $F$-blind, so the
count is monotone in $F$ and immune to later corruptions.
Only the $A$- and $B$-returns read the bound value.
A $C$-return reads none, so an instance in which every return is a $C$-return
need never bind.
At most $f$ processes are ever corrupted, so any such set of $f+1$ contains a
member outside the final corrupted set, hence a never-corrupted genuine caller.
That member is the witness which carries Validity from the bound value down to
a caller.
}

\dynamics{The coin and its parameters}{%
One coin resolution has four outcomes.
With probability $\varepsilon$ the outcome is the bit $0$, and with probability
$\varepsilon$ it is the bit $1$.
In either case every correct process receives that bit.
With mass $1 - 2\varepsilon - \failmass$ the outcome is $\top$, the unresolved
coin: each process may then be handed either bit by the adversary,
independently of the others.
With probability $\failmass$ the outcome is $\dagger$, on which the resolution
delivers to no process at all (D17).
The outcome $\dagger$ models a delivery failure of the implementation this
specification stands for.
That implementation reconstructs the coin from an $\varepsilon$-correct
verifiable secret sharing, and the reconstruction can fail; on failure the coin
delivers nothing.
The two non-bit outcomes are distinct at the interface.
On $\top$ the coin delivers, and the adversary only picks which bit each
process receives.
On $\dagger$ no delivery occurs, and the callers of that instance hold no
return to read.
Transition System~3 is a specification, not an implementation.
The $\varepsilon$-Goodness it asserts is an assumption of this development, to
be discharged by an implementation over Gather and SRSD that is not modelled
here.
The assumption is inert for the results proved: safety holds for every
admissible pair $\varepsilon, \failmass$, including $\varepsilon = 0$, where the
coin resolves to $\top$ or $\dagger$ almost surely.
Safety is insensitive to $\failmass$ for a further reason: the outcome $\dagger$
removes returns rather than adding any, and every safety property of this
development constrains the returns that do occur.
It is a termination argument that would consume the $\varepsilon$-mass, and
such an argument must carry the $\failmass$-mass too, since a $\dagger$ resolution
produces no return at all.
Termination and fairness are out of scope.
}

\begin{algorithm}

      form: the original carries a fifth round of echoes, and the return
      conditions here read one message level down. The theorems below concern

        \medskip
        \State send $\langle\mathrm{INPUT}, b\rangle$ to all processes
        \State \textbf{upon} receiving $\langle\mathrm{INPUT}, b\rangle$ from at

        \State\quad (b) receiving $\langle\mathrm{ECHO}, \cdot\rangle$ from at least
          $n - f$ processes and $|\mathit{Valid}| > 1$

        \State\quad (b) receiving $\langle\mathrm{VOTE}, \cdot\rangle$ from at least
          $n - f$ processes and $|\mathit{Valid}| > 1$

          to all \textbf{else} send $\langle\mathrm{BIND}, \bot\rangle$ to all
        \State \textbf{wait until}
        \State\quad (a) receiving $\langle\mathrm{BIND}, b\rangle$ from at least

        \State\quad (b) receiving $\langle\mathrm{BIND}, \cdot\rangle$ from at least
          $n - f$ processes and
        \State\quad there exists $b \in \{0,1\}$ such that $\langle\mathrm{BIND}, b\rangle$
          has been received once and
        \State\quad $\langle\mathrm{VOTE}, b\rangle$ has been received from $f + 1$

        \State\quad (c) receiving $\langle\mathrm{BIND}, \bot\rangle$ from at least
          $n - f$ processes and $|\mathit{Valid}| > 1$
        \State\quad \textbf{if} (a) \textbf{then} \textbf{return} $(b, A)$

\begin{definition}[The GBCA implementation instance]
  \label{def:aba-gbca-impl}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.implInst}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-labels, def:aba-params}
  The GBCA implementation of the source blueprint, after~\cite{ABDY22}, as a
  per-round PLTS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:gbca-impl}); see the caption of that
  algorithm for how the four-round form relates to the original.
  Each process runs a four-stage message exchange over an authenticated set-based network.
  On a $\mathrm{callG}$ it multicasts $\langle\mathrm{INPUT},b\rangle$; it relays $\langle\mathrm{INPUT},b\rangle$ once $f{+}1$ receipts of it arrive (the relay gate); it multicasts $\langle\mathrm{ECHO},b\rangle$ on an $n{-}f$ INPUT-$b$ quorum, $\langle\mathrm{VOTE},v\rangle$ on an $n{-}f$ ECHO quorum, and $\langle\mathrm{BIND},v\rangle$ on an $n{-}f$ VOTE quorum, each payload a real bit or $\bot$.
  A process returns grade $A\,b$ on an $n{-}f$ BIND-$b$ quorum, $B\,b$ on an $n{-}f$ any-BIND quorum containing $b$ with $f{+}1$ VOTE-$b$s and $|\mathrm{Valid}|>1$, and $C$ on an $n{-}f$ BIND-$\bot$ quorum with $|\mathrm{Valid}|>1$.
  The network is set-based and authenticated (D5): each sender owns one idempotent message set, the adversary schedules delivery ($\mathrm{deliver}$), and a corrupted $j\in F$ injects an arbitrary message ($\mathrm{byz}$).
  Protocol sends are gated on prior participation (D8).
  The state is the protocol's own data, and the three returns read nothing beyond the receipts Algorithm~\ref{alg:gbca-impl}'s three wait-until cases name.
\end{definition}

The Lean encoding of Algorithm~\ref{alg:gbca-impl} is the round-$r$ instance
$\mathrm{GBCA.implInst}$, an LTS over the shared label alphabet of the
development.
Fix the parameters $n$ and $f$ with $3f < n$.
Write $[n]$ for the process identifiers and $F \subseteq [n]$ for the corrupted
set.
The message type is
\[
  \mathrm{Msg} \;=\;
  \{\,\langle\mathrm{INPUT}, b\rangle,\ \langle\mathrm{ECHO}, b\rangle
    \;:\; b \in \{0,1\}\,\}
  \;\cup\;
  \{\,\langle\mathrm{VOTE}, v\rangle,\ \langle\mathrm{BIND}, v\rangle
    \;:\; v \in \{0,1,\bot\}\,\}.
\]
Every rule of the instance is Dirac.
A rule is therefore determined by its guard and its state update, and the table
below carries no distribution column.

The local state of one process is the record $\mathrm{ProcState}$ with fields
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathit{input}$ --- the bit supplied by the environment call; unset
    before the call.
  \item $\mathit{sentInput} : \{0,1\} \to \{\text{true},\text{false}\}$ --- which
    $\mathrm{INPUT}$ payloads this process has multicast.
  \item $\mathit{sentEcho}$ --- the $\mathrm{ECHO}$ payload multicast; unset if
    none, and written at most once.
  \item $\mathit{sentVote}$ --- the $\mathrm{VOTE}$ payload multicast; unset if
    none, and the recorded payload may itself be $\bot$.
  \item $\mathit{sentBind}$ --- the $\mathrm{BIND}$ payload multicast, on the
    same convention.
  \item $\mathit{returned}$ --- whether this process has returned from the
    instance.
\end{itemize}
The state of the instance is the record $\mathrm{ImplState}$ with fields
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathit{proc} : [n] \to \mathrm{ProcState}$ --- the local states.
  \item $\mathit{sent} : [n] \to \mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Msg})$ --- $\mathit{sent}(j)$
    is the set of messages $j$ has multicast (D5).
  \item $\mathit{recv} : [n] \times [n] \to \mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Msg})$ ---
    $\mathit{recv}(i,j)$ is the set of $j$'s messages the adversary has delivered
    to $i$ (D5).
  \item $F$ --- the corrupted set, held in lockstep with the other components by
    the $\mathrm{fail}$ broadcast.
\end{itemize}
Multicasts are idempotent and thresholds count distinct senders, so message
duplication and point-to-point scheduling are absorbed into the set model (D5).
Three derived quantities occur in the guards.
Set
\[
  \#_i(m) \;=\; |\{\, j \in [n] \;:\; m \in \mathit{recv}(i,j) \,\}|,
  \qquad
  \mathit{Val}_i \;=\;
    \{\, b \in \{0,1\} \;:\; \#_i(\langle\mathrm{INPUT}, b\rangle) \ge n - f \,\},
\]
and write $\#_i(\mathrm{EC})$, $\#_i(\mathrm{VT})$, $\#_i(\mathrm{BD})$ for the
payload-blind counts, that is, the number of distinct senders from which $i$
holds some $\mathrm{ECHO}$, some $\mathrm{VOTE}$, some $\mathrm{BIND}$.
The set $\mathit{Val}_i$ is Algorithm~\ref{alg:gbca-impl}'s variable
$\mathit{Valid}$, read off the delivered messages instead of being stored, so
the condition $|\mathit{Valid}| > 1$ becomes $\mathit{Val}_i = \{0,1\}$.
In the table $\mathrm{IN}\,b$, $\mathrm{EC}\,b$, $\mathrm{VT}\,v$ and
$\mathrm{BD}\,v$ abbreviate the four message forms, ``mcast'' abbreviates the
insertion of a message into the sender's $\mathit{sent}$ pool, and a dash marks
an unguarded rule.

The three \textbf{wait until} blocks of Algorithm~\ref{alg:gbca-impl} are the
guards of \texttt{voteBit}/\allowbreak\texttt{voteBot},
\texttt{bindBit}/\allowbreak\texttt{bindBot} and
\texttt{retA}/\allowbreak\texttt{retB}/\allowbreak\texttt{retC}; the write-once fields
$\mathit{sentEcho}$, $\mathit{sentVote}$, $\mathit{sentBind}$ carry the
``not sent yet'' side conditions.
The three return rules are the three wait-until cases of the last block
verbatim: case (a) is an $n{-}f$ $\mathrm{BIND}\,v$ quorum, case (b) an $n{-}f$
any-$\mathrm{BIND}$ quorum containing $\mathrm{BIND}\,v$ together with $f{+}1$
$\mathrm{VOTE}\,v$ receipts and $|\mathit{Valid}| > 1$, and case (c) an $n{-}f$
$\mathrm{BIND}\,\bot$ quorum with $|\mathit{Valid}| > 1$.
Each reads the receipts its own case names and nothing else, and case (c)
returns no bit.
The $bind$ and $grade$ fields of Transition System~2 are abstractions of these
receipt patterns and belong to the specification alone; the refinement below
supplies them from the receipts.

\begin{theorem}[GBCA instance refinement]
  \label{thm:gbca-inst-refines}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.implRefines}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-gbca-impl, def:aba-subspecs}
  The GBCA implementation instance is forward-simulated, instance by instance, by the GBCA specification instance.
  The relation reads the specification's $call$, $ret$ and $F$ off the implementation state and certifies its $bind$ and $grade$ by receipt quorums.
  Binding is deferred to the first return: an $A$- or $B$-return met by an unbound specification state is answered by the two-step weak burst $\mathrm{bindSet};\mathrm{ret}$, and a run whose returns are all $C$-returns never binds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-gbca-impl, def:aba-subspecs, def:forwardsim}
  We exhibit an LTS forward simulation of the implementation instance by the specification instance and check it constructor by constructor.
  The implementation state is the protocol's own data, so the specification's $call$, $ret$ and $F$ are read off it directly.
  Its $bind$ and $grade$ are not stored by the protocol, and the relation carries receipt evidence for them instead.
  A specification state bound to $v$ is certified by an $n{-}f$ $\mathrm{ECHO}\,v$ receipt quorum at some process.
  An $A$-side grade lock is certified by an $n{-}f$ $\mathrm{BIND}\,v$ receipt quorum for some bit $v$, a $C$-side lock by an $n{-}f$ $\mathrm{BIND}\,\bot$ quorum.
  All three certificates are $F$-blind and monotone in the delivered sets, so they survive every implementation step and every $\mathrm{fail}$.

  Consider an $A$- or $B$-return met by a specification state with $bind = \bot$.
  Its own guard yields the certificate.
  Case (a)'s $n{-}f$ $\mathrm{BIND}\,v$ quorum has more than $|F|$ senders, hence an honest one, whose $\mathrm{BIND}\,v$ is backed by an $n{-}f$ $\mathrm{VOTE}\,v$ quorum; case (b)'s $f{+}1$ $\mathrm{VOTE}\,v$ receipts likewise have an honest sender, whose $\mathrm{VOTE}\,v$ is backed by an $n{-}f$ $\mathrm{ECHO}\,v$ quorum.
  That certificate supplies both guards of $\mathrm{bindSet}$.
  Refine it once more to an $n{-}f$ $\mathrm{INPUT}\,v$ receipt quorum: its honest senders each hold an input (D8), which is the quorum guard, and $n - f \ge f + 1$ makes the same count the $f{+}1$ $\mathrm{SuppOK}(v)$ count (D15).
  The return is then answered by the weak transition $\mathrm{bindSet};\mathrm{retA}$, resp.\ $\mathrm{bindSet};\mathrm{retB}$, whose leading $\tau$-step is absorbed by the $\tau$-closure of the weak step.
  Every later return meets a bound specification state and is answered by a single specification return.

  Value agreement is the crux.
  At most one bit carries an $n{-}f$ $\mathrm{ECHO}$ receipt quorum.
  Take quorums for $v$ and for $v'$: they have $(n-f) + (n-f) - n = n - 2f > f \ge |F|$ senders in common, hence a common honest sender, and an honest sender's $\mathrm{ECHO}$ payload is the one held in its write-once $\mathit{sentEcho}$ field, so $v = v'$.
  The bit a return hands out therefore equals the bit already bound, which is what the $bind = v$ guards of $\mathrm{retA}$ and $\mathrm{retB}$ demand.
  A/C exclusivity is the same intersection argument one layer up, on the write-once $\mathit{sentBind}$ field: an $n{-}f$ $\mathrm{BIND}\,v$ quorum and an $n{-}f$ $\mathrm{BIND}\,\bot$ quorum would meet in an honest process holding two $\mathrm{BIND}$ payloads, which is a contradiction.
  This discharges the grade guard of $\mathrm{retA}$ against the $C$-side certificate and that of $\mathrm{retC}$ against the $A$-side certificate.

  A $C$-return needs no binding.
  Its specification guards are the two $f{+}1$ supports and the $C$-side grade lock, so it is answered by the single step $\mathrm{retC}$ from a bound and from an unbound state alike.
  Both supports come from its own $|\mathit{Valid}| > 1$ evidence: the $n{-}f$ $\mathrm{INPUT}$ receipt quorum for each bit sits at the returner itself, and $n - f \ge f + 1$.
  The $B$-return's dissent guard is the same harvest at the opposite bit.
  Each harvest passes through the invariant clause $input\_supp$: an honest multicast of $\langle\mathrm{INPUT},b\rangle$ is by a genuine holder of $b$, or already certifies $f{+}1$ distinct processes each a genuine holder of $b$ or corrupted.
  The clause is inductive because the first honest relayer's $f{+}1$ $\mathrm{INPUT}\,b$ receipt senders are each corrupted or genuine holders, and the count is monotone under every step.
  Transporting such a count to the specification side is one rewrite along $call = \mathit{input}$ and the aligned corrupted sets.
  A corruption row is answered by the specification's own $\mathrm{fail}$, and every delivery, relay and protocol-send row by a specification stutter.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Family congruence and the GBCA family refinement]
  \label{thm:gbca-family-refines}
  \lean{PLTS.ForwardSimulation.family}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.familyRefines}\leanok
  \uses{thm:gbca-inst-refines, def:idle-family}
  Per-instance forward simulations lift to the $\mathbb{N}$-indexed families
  (pointwise relation; broadcast labels matched by the abstract family's own
  broadcast under a corruption-compatibility lemma), and the lift to a
  probabilistic forward simulation is by the LTS bridge, once, at family
  level.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{thm:gbca-inst-refines, def:idle-family, lem:bridge}
  The per-instance simulations of Theorem~\ref{thm:gbca-inst-refines} lift to the $\mathbb{N}$-indexed families under the pointwise relation.
  Owned and silent labels move the one stepping instance and are matched instance-wise; a corruption broadcast is matched by the abstract family's own broadcast, using a corruption-compatibility lemma that the per-instance relation survives a synchronous $\mathrm{fail}$ at every instance.
  The families are LTS, so the LTS bridge (Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge}) turns the family-level LTS forward simulation into a probabilistic one, applied once at family level.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The ABA core}

\begin{algorithm}
    \caption{Implementation of ABA. Algorithm 1 of the source blueprint, after
      Abraham, Ben-David and Yandamuri \cite{ABDY22}}
    \label{alg:aba-core}
    \begin{algorithmic}
        \State \textbf{Input:} $b \in \{0,1\}$
        \State \textbf{Sub-protocols:} countably many instances of GBCA and WCC
        \medskip
        \State $r \gets 1$
        \State \textbf{loop}
        \State\quad $(b, g) \gets \mathrm{GBCA}_r(b)$
          \Comment{$b \in \{0,1,\bot\}$, $g \in \{A,B,C\}$}
        \State\quad $c \gets \mathrm{WCC}_r()$
        \State\quad \textbf{if} $b = \bot$:
        \State\quad\quad $b \gets c$
        \State\quad \textbf{elif} $g = A$:
        \State\quad\quad send $\langle\mathrm{DECIDED}, b\rangle$ to all
        \State\quad $r \gets r + 1$
        \medskip
        \State \textbf{upon} receiving $\langle\mathrm{DECIDED}, b\rangle$ from
          $f + 1$ processes and having not sent it:
        \State\quad send $\langle\mathrm{DECIDED}, b\rangle$ to all
        \State \textbf{upon} receiving $\langle\mathrm{DECIDED}, b\rangle$ from
          $n - f$ processes and having sent it:
        \State\quad \textbf{return} $b$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{definition}[The ABA core]
  \label{def:aba-core}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.core}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-labels}
  The core algorithm as a PLTS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:aba-core}).
  Each process runs a five-phase handshake cycle $\mathrm{idle} \to \mathrm{toCallG} \to \mathrm{awaitG} \to \mathrm{toCallW} \to \mathrm{awaitW}$ and back to $\mathrm{toCallG}$ of the next round, carrying an estimate adopted from the graded GBCA outcome or, when the estimate is $\bot$, from the coin.
  The per-round bookkeeping between the coin return and the next GBCA call is fused into the $\mathrm{retW}$ step (D10), which multicasts $\langle\mathrm{DECIDED}, b\rangle$ when the round's grade was $A\,b$.
  A DECIDED gossip layer pools the multicasts in per-process sent and received pools; a corrupted process may pool either bit and so equivocates at this layer (D12$'$).
  A process returns $\mathrm{retABA}\,id\,b$ once $n - f$ distinct senders of $b$ have been delivered to it and $b$ is in its own sent pool.
  Corrupted processes additionally carry Byzantine call- and return-driver rules at each handshake (D11).
\end{definition}

The idle self-loop of a process carries \texttt{inputLoop} together with the Byzantine driver rules \texttt{callGByz}, \texttt{retGByz}, \texttt{callWByz} and \texttt{retWByz}, which fire at any phase for $id \in F$ and change no state (D11).
The step \texttt{retG} sets $est$ to the grade, and \texttt{retW} increments the round, adopts the coin when $est = \bot$, and multicasts $\langle\mathrm{DECIDED},b\rangle$ on an $A$-grade return (D10).
The DECIDED pool layer is three $\tau$-loops, round-agnostic because the pools carry no round index: \texttt{deliver}, by which the adversary moves a sender's pooled $\langle\mathrm{DECIDED},b\rangle$ to a receiver; \texttt{echo}, the multicast on $f{+}1$ distinct receipts; and \texttt{byzDecided}, by which $id \in F$ pools either bit (D12$'$).
The exit \texttt{retABA}\,$id\,b$ fires once $n-f$ distinct senders of $b$ have been delivered to $id$ and $b$ is in $id$'s own sent pool.

\blueprintfig{fig-core-phases}{The ABA core: the per-process five-phase handshake cycle, the fused DECIDED multicast at the $A$-grade coin return (D10), and the DECIDED pool layer gating $\mathrm{retABA}$.}

The Lean encoding of Algorithm~\ref{alg:aba-core} is the system
$\mathrm{ABA.core}$, an LTS over the same label alphabet.
It is the round-loop control component only.
The two sub-protocols are separate systems, and the $\mathrm{callG}$,
$\mathrm{retG}$, $\mathrm{callW}$, $\mathrm{retW}$ steps of the core are pure
handshakes on the API labels.
Every rule is Dirac, so again the table needs no distribution column.

Each process carries a handshake phase, an element of
\[
  \mathrm{Phase} \;=\;
  \{\,\mathrm{idle},\ \mathrm{toCallG},\ \mathrm{awaitG},\ \mathrm{toCallW},\
    \mathrm{awaitW}\,\},
\]
traversed as
$\mathrm{idle} \to \mathrm{toCallG} \to \mathrm{awaitG} \to \mathrm{toCallW}
\to \mathrm{awaitW} \to \mathrm{toCallG} \to \cdots$.
There is no separate phase for the per-round bookkeeping between the WCC return
and the next GBCA call, because that bookkeeping is fused into the
$\mathrm{retW}$ step (D10).
The per-process record $\mathrm{ProcCore}$ has fields
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathit{input}$ --- the external input bit; unset before
    $\mathrm{callABA}$.
  \item $\mathit{est}$ --- the current estimate; the value $\bot$ is
    Algorithm~\ref{alg:aba-core}'s estimate awaiting the coin.
  \item $\mathit{round} \in \mathbb{N}$ --- the current round, counted from $0$
    (D9).
  \item $\mathit{phase} \in \mathrm{Phase}$ --- the handshake phase.
  \item $\mathit{lastGrade}$ --- the graded outcome of the current round's GBCA;
    unset before the return, and cleared by the round advance.
  \item $\mathit{returned}$ --- whether $\mathrm{retABA}$ has fired at this
    process.
\end{itemize}
The global record $\mathrm{CoreState}$ has fields
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathit{procs} : [n] \to \mathrm{ProcCore}$ --- the control states.
  \item $\mathit{decSent} : [n] \to \mathcal{P}(\{0,1\})$ ---
    $\mathit{decSent}_j$ is the set of $\mathrm{DECIDED}$ payloads $j$ has
    multicast (D12$'$).
  \item $\mathit{decRecv} : [n] \times [n] \to \mathcal{P}(\{0,1\})$ ---
    $\mathit{decRecv}(i,j)$ is the set of $j$'s payloads delivered to $i$
    (D12$'$).
  \item $F$ --- the corrupted set, held in lockstep by the $\mathrm{fail}$
    broadcast.
\end{itemize}
The pools are per process rather than per bit, so a corrupted process may hold
both payloads at once and deliver them selectively (D12$'$).
Write
\[
  \#_i(b) \;=\; |\{\, j \in [n] \;:\; b \in \mathit{decRecv}(i,j) \,\}|
\]
for the number of distinct senders whose $\langle\mathrm{DECIDED}, b\rangle$
has been delivered to $i$; this is the count the return guard reads.
Write $\mathrm{est}(A\,b) = \mathrm{est}(B\,b) = b$ and
$\mathrm{est}(C) = \bot$ for the estimate a graded outcome dictates.
In the table $\mathrm{DEC}\,b$ abbreviates $\langle\mathrm{DECIDED}, b\rangle$,
``mcast'' abbreviates the insertion of a payload into the sender's
$\mathit{decSent}$ pool, and a dash marks an unguarded rule.

Only the five Byzantine driver rules test membership of $F$; the other ten
rules apply to corrupted and honest processes alike.
The return rule \texttt{ret} is the notable case:
a corrupted process must pass the same $n - f$ quorum guard, exactly as in the
specification.
The corruption rule \texttt{fail} is a synchronisation of every component of the
composition, each keeping its own copy of $F$.
The $\mathrm{est}$-update of \texttt{retW} is Algorithm~\ref{alg:aba-core}'s
branch \textbf{if} $b = \bot$ \textbf{then} $b \gets c$, and the conditional
multicast is its branch \textbf{elif} $g = A$; the two are fused with the round
advance into one Dirac step (D10).
The \texttt{echo} guard is payload-specific, matching
Algorithm~\ref{alg:aba-core}'s clause ``having not sent it'': a pool already
holding the other payload does not block the echo of $b$.

\dynamics{The round loop}{%
Each process runs a five-phase cycle: idle, ready to call the round's GBCA,
awaiting its return, ready to call the round's coin, awaiting the coin.
The graded outcome fixes the next estimate, an $A$- or $B$-return of value $b$
setting it to $b$ and a $C$-return clearing it to $\bot$; a cleared estimate is
filled by the coin of the same round, and a set estimate ignores that coin.
When the round's grade was $A\,b$, the step that receives the coin also
multicasts $\langle \mathrm{DECIDED}, b \rangle$ and advances the round.
A process holding $\langle \mathrm{DECIDED}, b \rangle$ from $f+1$ distinct
senders and not having multicast $\langle \mathrm{DECIDED}, b \rangle$ itself multicasts
$\langle \mathrm{DECIDED}, b \rangle$ itself, the $f+1$ threshold guaranteeing
one honest sender behind any echo.
The return rule requires $\langle \mathrm{DECIDED}, b \rangle$ from $n-f$
distinct senders together with the returning process's own prior multicast
of $b$.
The $n-f$ count is what excludes disagreement: two returns of different bits
would need two sender sets of that size, and any two such sets meet in more
than $f$ processes, hence in an honest one.
The own-multicast clause makes every return the endpoint of a diffusion its
own holder joined.
That an honest process never pools both payloads is not a property of the core
in isolation --- nothing in its rules forbids two $A$-grades of opposite bits in
different rounds --- but of the composition, where Graded Agreement on the GBCA
family rules that pair out; the core alone guarantees only that each process
returns at most once, by the flag its return rule sets.
}

\subsection{The hybrids and substitution}

\begin{definition}[The hybrids]
  \label{def:aba-hybrids}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.hybridImpl}\lean{PLTS.ABA.hybridSpec}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-subspecs, def:aba-labels}
  The composed systems $\mathsf{GBCA} \parallel (\mathsf{Core} \parallel
  \mathsf{WCC.Spec})$ with the sub-protocol API hidden, at implementation and
  specification level for the GBCA slot respectively. GBCA occupies the
  refinable slot of the parallel composition.
\end{definition}

\blueprintfig{fig-hybrid}{The hybrids: the composition $\mathsf{GBCA} \parallel (\mathsf{Core} \parallel \mathsf{WCC.Spec})$ under $\mathrm{abstract}\,\mathrm{hiddenAPI}$.
The GBCA family box is the refinable slot, carrying either the implementation family or the specification family (the substitution); the Core and WCC-specification family boxes are fixed.
One illustrated rendezvous $\mathrm{callG}\,r\,id\,b$: the Core emits it, instance $r$ steps on it, and every other instance idles.
The $\mathrm{abstract}\,\mathrm{hiddenAPI}$ frame turns the GBCA/WCC handshake alphabet into $\tau$; the visible API $\mathrm{callABA}/\mathrm{retABA}/\mathrm{fail}$ leaves the frame.}

The case study composes the core with two sub-protocol families over the single
alphabet $\mathrm{Lab}(n)$.
Parallel composition is full synchronisation: on a visible label both components
step simultaneously and the successor is the independent product, while on
$\tau$ exactly one component steps and the other holds its state.
An $\mathbb{N}$-indexed family behaves as follows.
On $\tau$, and on a label owned by instance $r$, only that instance steps.
On $\mathrm{fail}(i)$ every instance applies the corruption transform.
On any other visible label the family idles in place.
Abstraction $\mathrm{abstract}_H$ relabels every $H$-labelled transition to
$\tau$ and leaves the remaining transitions untouched.

Fix the context
\[
  \mathrm{context}(P) \;=\;
    \mathrm{core}(P) \parallel \mathrm{WCC.specFamily}(P),
\]
which holds WCC at specification level.
The two hybrids are
\[
  \mathrm{hybridImpl}(P) \;=\; \mathrm{abstract}_H\bigl(
    \mathrm{GBCA.implFamily}(P) \parallel \mathrm{context}(P) \bigr),
\]
\[
  \mathrm{hybridSpec}(P) \;=\; \mathrm{abstract}_H\bigl(
    \mathrm{GBCA.specFamily}(P) \parallel \mathrm{context}(P) \bigr),
\]
that is, writing the composition tree out,
\[
  \mathrm{abstract}_H\Bigl(
    \mathrm{GBCA.implFamily}(P) \parallel
      \bigl(\mathrm{core}(P) \parallel \mathrm{WCC.specFamily}(P)\bigr)\Bigr)
\]
and the same tree with $\mathrm{GBCA.implFamily}(P)$ replaced by
$\mathrm{GBCA.specFamily}(P)$.
The state space of $\mathrm{hybridImpl}(P)$ is accordingly
\[
  \bigl(\mathbb{N} \to \mathrm{GBCA.ImplState}\bigr) \times
  \bigl(\mathrm{CoreState} \times
    (\mathbb{N} \to \mathrm{WCC.SpecState})\bigr),
\]
and that of $\mathrm{hybridSpec}(P)$ replaces the first factor by
$\mathbb{N} \to \mathrm{GBCA.SpecState}$.

The GBCA family occupies the left argument of the outer parallel composition.
That is the refinable slot: the right argument $\mathrm{context}(P)$ is held
fixed, and it is the only slot in which the two hybrids differ.
Substituting the specification family for the implementation family is therefore
one application of the precongruence for parallel composition against a fixed
right-hand system, followed by one application of the precongruence for
abstraction.
No transitivity of simulations enters.

The hidden alphabet is the sub-protocol API,
\[
  H \;=\; \{\, l \;:\; \mathrm{gbcaRound}(l) \ne \bot
    \ \text{ or }\ \mathrm{wccRound}(l) \ne \bot \,\},
\]
which is exactly the set of labels
\[
  \{\, \mathrm{callG}(r,i,b),\ \mathrm{retG}(r,i,g),\
    \mathrm{callW}(r,i),\ \mathrm{retW}(r,i,c) \,\},
\]
where $r$ ranges over $\mathbb{N}$, $i$ over $[n]$, the bits $b$ and $c$ over
$\{0,1\}$, and $g$ over the graded outcomes
$\{A\,0, A\,1, B\,0, B\,1, C\}$.
Every one of these is mapped to $\tau$ by $\mathrm{abstract}_H$.
What remains visible is $\tau$ itself together with the ABA interface and
corruption, namely $\mathrm{callABA}(i,b)$, $\mathrm{retABA}(i,b)$ and
$\mathrm{fail}(i)$.
The two hybrids are thus compared on the ABA interface alone.

\begin{theorem}[Substitution]
  \label{thm:aba-substitution}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.substSim}\lean{PLTS.ABA.substitution}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-hybrids, thm:gbca-family-refines}
  Replacing the GBCA implementation family by its specification family under
  the fixed context is a probabilistic forward simulation — the parallel and
  abstraction precongruences applied to the family refinement — and hence a
  trace-distribution inclusion, by soundness.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-hybrids, thm:gbca-family-refines, thm:forward-sound}
  Apply the parallel and abstraction precongruences to the family refinement of Theorem~\ref{thm:gbca-family-refines}: parallel composition with the fixed context $\mathsf{Core} \parallel \mathsf{WCC.Spec}$ and then hiding the sub-protocol API each preserve probabilistic forward simulation.
  The result is a probabilistic forward simulation of the implementation-side hybrid by the specification-side hybrid.
  Soundness (Theorem~\ref{thm:forward-sound}) turns it into the trace-distribution inclusion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The core simulation and the main theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The core simulation]
  \label{thm:aba-coresim}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.coreSim}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-hybrids, def:aba-spec}
  The specification-side hybrid is forward-simulated by the repaired ABA specification under the relation $Inv \wedge Abs$.
  The abstraction $Abs$ pins the corrupted set $F$, the returns, and $coin = \bot$ — the twin never fires the coin rule — and is two-phase on the twin's $val$.
  In phase 1, before the first return, the twin is unbound: its abstract calls equal the concrete write-once inputs and its ghost is synced.
  In phase 2, after the first return, the board is clear and $bind = val = some\,v$ with $v$ certified by a concrete A-lock.
  Every hidden row and every concrete coin flip is answered by a stutter under a constant coupling.
  The only burst is $\mathrm{decide\_burst}$ at the first $\mathrm{retABA}$: within one weak step it binds, fills, decides, and returns.
  The concrete invariant carries the cross-round Graded Agreement machinery (downward C-lock propagation, upward bind commitment).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-hybrids, def:aba-spec, def:weakstep}
  We check the relation $Inv \wedge Abs$ row by row, one step class at a time, verifying that $Inv$ is preserved at each.
  Every hidden row and the coin flip are answered by an $Abs$-frame stutter: the twin holds still under a constant coupling, and since $Abs$ pins $coin = \bot$ the twin never fires the coin rule, so the probabilistic coin row couples to the stutter.
  The first $\mathrm{retABA}$ is answered by the single $\mathrm{decide\_burst}$ weak step, which within one weak transition binds, fills the board, decides, and returns.
  The bind is licensed by the $f{+}1$ input support carried in the invariant; the $n-f$ DECIDED tally behind the return is what a pigeonhole turns into a never-corrupted sender whose pooled bit certifies the $A$-lock.
  A $\mathrm{fail}$ row is answered by the corruption broadcast.
  The concrete invariant supplies the cross-round Graded Agreement machinery --- downward $C$-lock propagation and upward bind commitment --- that certifies the value bound at the burst.
\end{proof}

\blueprintfig{fig-coresim}{The core forward simulation: the two-phase abstract twin, its stutter answering every concrete row --- including the probabilistic coin flip --- under a constant coupling, and the single $\mathrm{decide\_burst}$ weak step aligned under the first $\mathrm{retABA}$.}

\dynamics{What the simulation buys}{%
The abstract twin of a hybrid state is lazy.
Every hidden row is answered by a stutter: the handshakes, the internal steps
of the sub-protocol specifications, and the delivery and echo of DECIDED all
leave the twin where it was.
The probabilistic coin row is answered the same way, under the constant
coupling that sends every outcome of the flip to the one unchanged twin, so the
twin never fires its own coin rule.
The entire decision is deferred to the first visible return, where a single
weak step binds, clears the call row and writes the decision value before the
return label is emitted.
The payoff comes from the soundness theorem for probabilistic forward
simulation: every achievable trace distribution of the composed implementation
is an achievable trace distribution of the specification.
A safety property proved of the small specification therefore holds of every
trace of positive probability in the composed implementation.
}

\begin{theorem}[Refinement and correctness of ABA]
  \label{thm:aba-main}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.refines}\lean{PLTS.ABA.main}\leanok
  \uses{thm:aba-substitution, thm:aba-coresim, thm:aba-spec-safe}
  Every trace distribution achievable by the implementation-side hybrid is
  achievable by the ABA specification (soundness applied to the substitution
  and core simulations separately, chained by transitivity of set inclusion —
  the transitivity of probabilistic forward simulation is not used), and
  hence every positive-probability trace of the implementation-side hybrid
  satisfies Validity and Agreement. Machine-checked axiom-clean; the composed
  simulation is additionally provided via
  Theorem~\ref{thm:forward-trans} and is likewise machine-checked axiom-clean,
  documented by a pinned axiom guard.\lean{PLTS.ABA.simComposed}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{thm:aba-substitution, thm:aba-coresim, thm:aba-spec-safe, thm:trace-support, thm:forward-sound}
  Soundness (Theorem~\ref{thm:forward-sound}) applied to the substitution simulation (Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-substitution}) and to the core simulation (Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-coresim}) gives two trace-distribution inclusions.
  Chaining them by transitivity of set inclusion yields $\operatorname{ATD}(\mathrm{hybridImpl}) \subseteq \operatorname{ATD}(\mathrm{spec})$, so every trace distribution achievable by the implementation-side hybrid is achievable by the specification.
  By the trace-support bridge (Theorem~\ref{thm:trace-support}), safety of the specification's trace supports (Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-spec-safe}) transfers along this inclusion, so every positive-probability trace of the implementation-side hybrid satisfies Validity and Agreement.
  The composed simulation is obtained separately, by transitivity of probabilistic forward simulation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The per-process presentation}
\label{sec:aba-perproc}

Both implementation-level encodings of this section are monolithic.
Each is a single automaton whose state is the whole system, so its rules may read fields of processes other than the one they move.
The GBCA delivery rule inspects the sender's message pool and the receiver's row in one guard, and the core's DECIDED delivery does the same.
That the guards are nevertheless local is a property of the rules and not of the state shape, and it is stated here as a theorem.
Each monolithic system is equal, on achievable trace distributions, to the full-synchronisation composition of $n$ automata, one per process, communicating only through delivery labels that the composition hides.
That composition is the product of Definition~\ref{def:syncproduct}, taken over the extended alphabet of Definition~\ref{def:relabel} and read back along the restriction that definition supplies.

\subsubsection{The per-layer automata and their theorems}

\begin{definition}[The per-process GBCA instance]
  \label{def:aba-gbca-perproc}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.GNet}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.ProcNode}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.ProcStep}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.procInst}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.netLabels}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.perProcInst}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-gbca-impl, def:relabel, def:syncproduct}
  The state of process $j$ is the node $\mathrm{ProcNode}$: the local record $proc$, the outbox $out$, the inbox rows $inbox(k)$ indexed by sender, and $j$'s own copy of the corrupted set $F$.
  No field of the node names another process's record or pools.
  The automaton runs over the extended alphabet $\mathrm{Lab}(n) \oplus \mathrm{GNet}(n)$, where $\mathrm{net}(i,j,m)$ is the delivery of $m$ from sender $j$ to receiver $i$.
  Its nineteen rules split the alphabet by role.
  On the calls and returns it owns, $\mathrm{callG}\,r\,j\,b$ and $\mathrm{retG}\,r\,j\,g$, it runs the rule of that name in Algorithm~\ref{alg:gbca-impl}, together with the input-enabledness loop; on a call or return addressed to another process it idles.
  On $\mathrm{fail}(k)$ every automaton applies the corruption transform to its own copy of $F$, which is what keeps the copies equal.
  On $\mathrm{net}(i,j,m)$ the sender supplies the outbox guard $m \in out$ and does not move, the receiver files $m$ under row $j$ and carries no outbox guard, a process that is both supplies the guard and files the message, and a process that is neither idles.
  The internal rules of Algorithm~\ref{alg:gbca-impl} --- the INPUT relay, the ECHO, VOTE and BIND multicasts, and the Byzantine injection of D5 --- carry $\tau$.
  Every guard reads the node alone.
  The composite is
  \[
    \mathrm{perProcInst}(P,r) \;=\;
      \mathrm{relabel}\Bigl(
        \mathrm{abstract}_{\mathrm{netLabels}}\bigl(
          \mathrm{syncProduct}_{j \in [n]}\ \mathrm{procInst}(P,r,j)
        \bigr)\Bigr),
  \]
  a system over $\mathrm{Lab}(n)$ with state space $[n] \to \mathrm{ProcNode}(n)$, where $\mathrm{netLabels}$ is the set of $\mathrm{net}(i,j,m)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The per-process core]
  \label{def:aba-core-perproc}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.DNet}\lean{PLTS.ABA.CoreNode}\lean{PLTS.ABA.CoreProcStep}\lean{PLTS.ABA.CoreProc}\lean{PLTS.ABA.gossipLabels}\lean{PLTS.ABA.perProcCore}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-core, def:relabel, def:syncproduct}
  The state of core process $j$ is the node $\mathrm{CoreNode}$: the control record $proc$, $j$'s own DECIDED pool $decOut$, the rows $decIn(k)$ of DECIDED receipts addressed to $j$, and $j$'s copy of $F$.
  Deviation D12$'$ is what admits the split: the DECIDED pools of Definition~\ref{def:aba-core} are already indexed by process, so $decOut$ is one process's field and $decIn$ one process's rows.
  The extended alphabet is $\mathrm{Lab}(n) \oplus \mathrm{DNet}(n)$, where $\mathrm{net}(i,j,b)$ carries $j$'s $\langle\mathrm{DECIDED},b\rangle$ to $i$.
  Its twenty-four rules split the alphabet by role.
  Process $j$ owns $\mathrm{callABA}(j,b)$, $\mathrm{retABA}(j,b)$ and the four round-tagged handshake labels naming $j$, and runs the rule of Definition~\ref{def:aba-core} at each, including the Byzantine driver rules of D11; on the same labels naming another process it idles.
  On $\mathrm{fail}(k)$ every automaton corrupts its own copy of $F$.
  The DECIDED echo and the Byzantine DECIDED injection carry $\tau$.
  On $\mathrm{net}(i,j,b)$ the sender contributes the soundness guard $b \in decOut$ and does not move, and the receiver contributes the freshness guard $b \notin decIn(j)$ and the write.
  Every guard reads the node alone, and no guard of Definition~\ref{def:aba-core} is otherwise altered.
  The composite is
  \[
    \mathrm{perProcCore}(P) \;=\;
      \mathrm{relabel}\Bigl(
        \mathrm{abstract}_{\mathrm{gossipLabels}}\bigl(
          \mathrm{syncProduct}_{j \in [n]}\ \mathrm{CoreProc}(P,j)
        \bigr)\Bigr),
  \]
  a system over $\mathrm{Lab}(n)$ with state space $[n] \to \mathrm{CoreNode}(n)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The per-process GBCA instance is the implementation instance]
  \label{thm:aba-gbca-perproc}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.perProcRefines}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.implRefinesPerProc}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.perProcInst_atd}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-gbca-perproc, def:aba-gbca-impl, def:achievable}
  For all parameters $P$ and all rounds $r$,
  \[
    \operatorname{ATD}(\mathrm{perProcInst}(P,r)) \;=\;
    \operatorname{ATD}(\mathrm{implInst}(P,r)) .
  \]
  Each inclusion is an LTS forward simulation along the packing map $\mathrm{unpack}$, and the step matching is one-to-one in both directions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-gbca-perproc, def:aba-gbca-impl, def:relabel, def:syncproduct, def:achievable, thm:forward-sound}
  Define the packing map $\mathrm{unpack}(q)(j) = (proc\,j,\ sent\,j,\ recv\,j,\ F)$ and take both relations to be its graph, which holds at the initial states.
  It makes every node's copy of the corrupted set equal to the monolithic $F$, and the $\mathrm{fail}$ broadcast preserves that equality, since every component applies the same corruption transform to its own copy.
  The step matching is one-to-one and uses no stuttering in either direction.
  A hidden rendezvous $\mathrm{net}(i,j,m)$ matches $\mathrm{deliver}\,i\,j\,m$, the sender component supplying the outbox guard and the receiver component the row update, every other component idle.
  An interleaved $\tau$ of one component matches the monolithic rule of the same name.
  A synchronised visible step matches the monolithic rule with the same guards, the counts taken over a packed node's rows being the monolithic counts.
  Both systems are LTS, so each matching is a probabilistic forward simulation, and soundness (Theorem~\ref{thm:forward-sound}) applied in each direction gives the two inclusions.
\end{proof}

Both composites of Definitions~\ref{def:aba-gbca-perproc} and~\ref{def:aba-core-perproc} have the same shape.
The frame is $\mathrm{relabel}$ composed with $\mathrm{abstract}_{\mathrm{netLabels}}$: it turns every $\mathrm{net}(i,j,m)$ into $\tau$, and what leaves it is the visible API $\mathrm{callG}$, $\mathrm{retG}$ and $\mathrm{fail}$ together with $\tau$.
At a rendezvous $\mathrm{net}(i,j,m)$ the sender $j$ supplies the guard $m \in \mathrm{out}_j$ and does not move, the receiver $i$ files $m$ under row $j$, and every other component answers with an idle self-loop, so full synchronisation moves exactly the two participants.
On a visible label every component steps, its owner by its own rule and the rest by an idle loop; on $\mathrm{fail}$ every copy of the corrupted set is corrupted.
The core composite carries the same description with $\mathrm{CoreProc}(P,j)$, $\mathrm{net}(i,j,b)$ and $\mathrm{gossipLabels}$ in place of the GBCA names.

The dashed vertical is Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-gbca-perproc}: the composite and the monolithic instance achieve the same trace distributions, the relation being the graph of $\mathrm{unpack}$.}

\begin{theorem}[The per-process core is the core]
  \label{thm:aba-core-perproc}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.CoreRel}\lean{PLTS.ABA.perProcCore_sim}\lean{PLTS.ABA.core_sim}\lean{PLTS.ABA.perProcCore_atd}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-core-perproc, def:aba-core, def:achievable}
  For all parameters $P$,
  \[
    \operatorname{ATD}(\mathrm{perProcCore}(P)) \;=\; \operatorname{ATD}(\mathrm{core}(P)) .
  \]
  Each inclusion is an LTS forward simulation along the packing relation $\mathrm{CoreRel}$, and the step matching is one-to-one in both directions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-core-perproc, def:aba-core, def:relabel, def:syncproduct, def:achievable, thm:forward-sound}
  Take the packing relation $\mathrm{CoreRel}$, which holds of a tuple $q$ and a monolithic state $s$ when $(q\,k).proc = procs\,k$, $(q\,k).decOut = decidedSent\,k$, $(q\,i).decIn\,k = decidedRecv\,i\,k$ and $(q\,k).F = s.F$ for all $i$ and $k$.
  Its last clause is the lockstep invariant on the corrupted set; the $\mathrm{fail}$ broadcast preserves it, every component answering that label with the same transform.
  It holds at the initial states, where the records are idle and the pools empty.
  Each rule of Definition~\ref{def:aba-core} is answered by one composite step, with no stuttering: on a visible label the owner moves and the rest idle, the DECIDED echo and the Byzantine injection are interleaved $\tau$-steps of one component, and $\mathrm{deliver}\,i\,j\,b$ is the hidden rendezvous $\mathrm{net}(i,j,b)$ with its two guards split between the sender and the receiver.
  Conversely each composite step is inverted by label class, and each class supplies exactly one monolithic rule.
  A label no component owns is blocked by the product, which is how the composite reproduces a monolithic guard that fails.
  Both systems are LTS, so each matching is a probabilistic forward simulation, and soundness (Theorem~\ref{thm:forward-sound}) applied in each direction gives the two inclusions.
\end{proof}

\dynamics{What the presentation certifies}{%
Calling either encoding a distributed algorithm is a claim about which state a
rule is allowed to read.
The monolithic encoding is the composed system with the tuple of nodes packed
into one record and the rendezvous already resolved, and Theorems
\ref{thm:aba-gbca-perproc} and~\ref{thm:aba-core-perproc} say that the packing
costs no observable behaviour in either direction.
Since every rule of the per-process automata is a function of one node, no rule
of either implementation reads across a process boundary.
The theorems also fix what stays centralized.
The corruption budget is the one piece of shared state: the guard
$k \notin F \wedge |F| < f$ is evaluated on each node's own copy of $F$, and
the copies agree only because $\mathrm{fail}$ is a broadcast every component
answers.
That is the adversary's bookkeeping and not the protocol's.
Nothing else is shared.
The message pools, the receipt rows and the control records each belong to one
process, and every quorum count is taken over one node's rows.
}

\subsubsection{The assembled hybrid}

The two theorems above treat one layer at a time, and each leaves the layer boundary of Definition~\ref{def:aba-hybrids} in place.
Two statements close the presentation.
The first substitutes both per-process layers into the hybrid at once, so that every implemented box of the composition is a composition of one automaton per process.
The second removes the boundary: the round loop of a process and its graded-agreement stages become a single automaton, and the hybrid becomes $n$ programs beside the coin oracle.
Both rest on the functional matchings of Lemma~\ref{def:strongfunctional}, in which the abstract state is a function of the concrete one.

\begin{theorem}[The assembled hybrid]
  \label{thm:aba-assembly}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.perProcGBCAFamily}\lean{PLTS.ABA.perProcContext}\lean{PLTS.ABA.hybridPerProc}\lean{PLTS.ABA.atd_parallel_left}\lean{PLTS.ABA.hybridPerProc_atd}\lean{PLTS.ABA.hybridPerProc_safe}\leanok
  \uses{thm:aba-gbca-perproc, thm:aba-core-perproc, def:aba-hybrids, def:achievable, def:strongfunctional}
  Let $\mathrm{perProcGBCAFamily}(P)$ be the $\mathbb{N}$-indexed family of the instances $\mathrm{perProcInst}(P,r)$, and let
  \[
    \mathrm{perProcContext}(P) \;=\;
      \mathrm{perProcCore}(P) \parallel \mathrm{WCC.specFamily}(P)
  \]
  be the context of Definition~\ref{def:aba-hybrids} with its core in per-process form.
  Set
  \[
    \mathrm{hybridPerProc}(P) \;=\; \mathrm{abstract}_H\bigl(
      \mathrm{perProcGBCAFamily}(P) \parallel \mathrm{perProcContext}(P)\bigr).
  \]
  Then $\operatorname{ATD}(\mathrm{hybridPerProc}(P)) = \operatorname{ATD}(\mathrm{hybridImpl}(P))$, and every trace of positive probability under an achievable trace distribution of $\mathrm{hybridPerProc}(P)$ satisfies Validity and Agreement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{thm:aba-gbca-perproc, thm:aba-core-perproc, thm:precong-parallel, thm:precong-abstract, thm:forward-sound, thm:aba-main, def:strongfunctional, def:idle-family}
  The two per-instance GBCA matchings of Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-gbca-perproc} lift to the two families by the family congruence, whose broadcast hypothesis is that packing commutes with the corruption transform on $\mathrm{fail}(k)$, the one label the family broadcasts.
  Both families are LTS, so each lifted matching is a probabilistic forward simulation, and so are the two core matchings of Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-core-perproc}.
  Each of the four is transported into the composition by the precongruences for parallel composition (Theorem~\ref{thm:precong-parallel}) and for abstraction (Theorem~\ref{thm:precong-abstract}); the core occupies the right factor, so its two transports are the parallel precongruence conjugated by the coordinate exchange of Lemma~\ref{def:strongfunctional}.
  Soundness (Theorem~\ref{thm:forward-sound}) turns each transport into a trace-distribution inclusion, and the four chain by transitivity of $\subseteq$ into the two inclusions of the equality.
  The coin oracle is never simulated: it is the fixed context of every precongruence step, which is what admits the argument although it is the one factor that is not an LTS.
  Safety is Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-main} read along the equality.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{The flat presentation}

\begin{definition}[The program of one process]
  \label{def:aba-flat}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.Flat.FlatNet}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Flat.FLab}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Flat.ABANode}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Flat.ABAProcStep}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Flat.ABAProc}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Flat.flatNetLabels}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Flat.flatGroup}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Flat.flatPre}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Flat.flatHybrid}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-core-perproc, def:aba-gbca-perproc, def:relabel, def:syncproduct, def:aba-hybrids}
  The state of process $j$ is the node $\mathrm{ABANode}(n) = \mathrm{CoreNode}(n) \times (\mathbb{N} \to \mathrm{ProcNode}(n))$: the round-loop node of Definition~\ref{def:aba-core-perproc} together with one graded-agreement stage of Definition~\ref{def:aba-gbca-perproc} per round.
  The automaton $\mathrm{ABAProc}(P,j)$ runs over the extended alphabet $\mathrm{Lab}(n) \oplus \mathrm{FlatNet}(n)$, where $\mathrm{FlatNet}$ carries both networks: $\mathrm{gnet}(r,i,j,m)$ is the round-$r$ stage delivery of $m$ from $j$ to $i$, and $\mathrm{dnet}(i,j,b)$ the delivery of $j$'s $\langle\mathrm{DECIDED},b\rangle$ to $i$.
  Its forty-one rules split the alphabet by role, and every transition is Dirac.
  On $\mathrm{callABA}(j,b)$ and $\mathrm{retABA}(j,b)$ only the round-loop half moves and no stage does.
  The two sub-protocol handshakes of round $r$ are the labels on which the round-loop half and the round-$r$ stage of the \emph{same} process move together, so each of them is one fused rule.
  A $\mathrm{callG}\,r\,j\,b$ fuses one of the two round-loop behaviours, honest or corrupted, with one of the two stage behaviours, the genuine call or the input-enabledness loop, which is four rules; a $\mathrm{retG}\,r\,j\,g$ fuses the two round-loop behaviours with the stage evidence that fixes the grade, which is six.
  The $\mathrm{retG}$ rules are the only ones in which stage evidence guards a visible label.
  On $\mathrm{callW}\,r\,j$ and $\mathrm{retW}\,r\,j\,c$ the automaton carries the round loop's half alone, the oracle's half being supplied at the outer composition.
  On $\mathrm{fail}(k)$ the round-loop copy of the corrupted set and every stage copy are written at once.
  Nine rules carry $\tau$: the DECIDED echo and the corrupted DECIDED injection of the round loop, and seven stage rules at an arbitrary round.
  Each of the two networks is a two-party rendezvous split into the self, sender, receiver and bystander roles, eight rules in all.
  Their guards differ by design: a DECIDED delivery is refused when the receiver already holds that payload from that sender, whereas a stage delivery carries no such guard, stage delivery being idempotent.
  A Byzantine guard is read off the copy of the corrupted set belonging to the half it drives.
  Every guard reads $j$'s node alone, and a label that $j$ does not own carries an idle self-loop, so full synchronisation lets that label's owner move.
  The composites are
  \[
    \mathrm{flatGroup}(P) \;=\;
      \mathrm{relabel}\Bigl(
        \mathrm{abstract}_{\mathrm{flatNetLabels}}\bigl(
          \mathrm{syncProduct}_{j \in [n]}\ \mathrm{ABAProc}(P,j)
        \bigr)\Bigr),
  \]
  a system over $\mathrm{Lab}(n)$ with state space $[n] \to \mathrm{ABANode}(n)$, and
  \[
    \mathrm{flatHybrid}(P) \;=\; \mathrm{abstract}_H\bigl(
      \mathrm{flatGroup}(P) \parallel \mathrm{WCC.specFamily}(P) \bigr),
  \]
  whose argument is written $\mathrm{flatPre}(P)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[ABA is $n$ programs and a coin oracle]
  \label{thm:aba-flat}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.Flat.unflat}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Flat.flatSim}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Flat.flatSimConverse}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Flat.flatABA_atd}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Flat.flatABA_safe}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-flat, def:aba-hybrids, def:achievable}
  For all parameters $P$,
  \[
    \operatorname{ATD}(\mathrm{flatHybrid}(P)) \;=\; \operatorname{ATD}(\mathrm{hybridImpl}(P)),
  \]
  and every trace of positive probability under an achievable trace distribution of $\mathrm{flatHybrid}(P)$ satisfies Validity and Agreement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-flat, def:strongfunctional, thm:precong-abstract, thm:forward-sound, thm:aba-main, def:achievable}
  Both systems of the statement are abstractions of the same shape, so it suffices to match $\mathrm{flatPre}(P)$ with $\mathrm{GBCA.implFamily}(P) \parallel (\mathrm{core}(P) \parallel \mathrm{WCC.specFamily}(P))$.
  Define the unflattening map $\mathrm{unflat}$: it splits each array of a monolithic state along the process index, giving process $x$ the round-loop record, DECIDED pool and receipt rows of $x$ together with $x$'s row of every round's stage, and it leaves the coin oracle's coordinate untouched.
  Every transition of either system other than the coin resolution is Dirac, and a transition and its image have the same successor, so $\mathrm{unflat}$ both commutes with steps and reflects them; the coin resolution appears on the two sides as the same pushforward of $\mathrm{wccPMF}$ and is matched by itself.
  Lemma~\ref{def:strongfunctional} therefore gives a probabilistic forward simulation along the graph of $\mathrm{unflat}$ and a second along its converse, neither system being an LTS.
  The map is not surjective --- a flat state whose copies of the corrupted set disagree has no preimage --- which is why the backward direction is the converse of the graph and not the graph of an inverse.
  Abstraction (Theorem~\ref{thm:precong-abstract}) transports both matchings, soundness (Theorem~\ref{thm:forward-sound}) turns each into a trace-distribution inclusion, and antisymmetry of $\subseteq$ gives the equality.
  Safety is Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-main} read along the equality.
\end{proof}

Each program holds one process's round loop and its stage of every round, and every guard of its forty-one rules reads that program's own node.

\dynamics{What is distributed and what is not}{%
The flat presentation states the read-access claim in its sharpest form.
Every implemented part of the protocol is $n$ programs.
The round loop, the graded-agreement stage of each round, the DECIDED gossip and
the stage network all sit inside $\mathrm{ABAProc}(P,j)$, and every guard of its
forty-one rules is a function of $j$'s node.
Two things stay centralized.
The first is the corruption budget, exactly as in the layer theorems: the guard
$k \notin F \wedge |F| < f$ is evaluated on each program's own copy of $F$, and
the copies agree only because $\mathrm{fail}$ is a broadcast every program
answers in lockstep.
That is the adversary's bookkeeping and not the protocol's.
The second is the coin oracle, and it is centralized of necessity.
An $\varepsilon$-common coin is a correlation between processes: the assumption
is that with probability at least $\varepsilon$ the processes of a round read the
same bit.
A correlation is not a property of any one process's state, so no assignment of
the oracle's transitions to per-process rules expresses it.
Writing the oracle as $n$ programs would mean supplying the protocol that
produces the correlation, which is the layer this development assumes rather
than proves.
It is also the one factor whose transitions are not Dirac, which is why it
survives both arguments untouched, as a fixed context in
Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-assembly} and as a matched coordinate in
Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-flat}.
}
