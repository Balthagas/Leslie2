\section{Systems, executions, and trace distributions}

A PLTS is the single model every system of this development is written in.
Executions record whole runs and traces record what is observable of them, and that separation is what lets an implementation be compared with its specification.
A refinement claim here is always an inclusion $\operatorname{ATD}(sys_C) \subseteq \operatorname{ATD}(sys_A)$ of achievable trace distributions, and safety properties are read off the supports of those distributions.
Throughout, $\tau$ is the distinguished silent label of the label type, a label is internal when it equals $\tau$ and external otherwise, $\delta_s$ is the Dirac distribution on $s$, $\mu \triangleright g$ is the pushforward of $\mu$ along $g$, and $s[x := v]$ is record update.
See \leanmodule{Leslie2/Systems/System} and \leanmodule{Leslie2/Systems/Trace}.

\section{Weak transitions and the two constructions}

Weak transitions absorb internal computation, and all three of them move a distribution over states rather than a state and run until an internal scheduler halts.
The two constructions closing the section turn those notions into ordinary systems --- the weak closure $\cdot^w$ makes a weak transition a single step, and the distribution-monad lift $\mathcal{D}(\cdot)$ makes a distribution over states a single state --- and the soundness proof of Section~\ref{sec:soundness} factors through their composite.

\section{Probabilistic simulations}

A simulation of a concrete system by an abstract one is carried by a relation together with a step-matching condition, phrased through a coupling of the two successor distributions.
The two notions of this section differ only in what the abstract side may answer a concrete step with: a single step for strong simulation, and a weak transition out of a belief distribution over abstract states for forward simulation.
Every refinement of the case study is proved in the second of them, whose belief distribution is where an abstract commitment is deferred to.

\section{Soundness of probabilistic forward simulation}
\label{sec:soundness}

Forward simulation is definitionally a strong simulation into $\mathcal{D}(sys_A^w)$, and soundness is that observation together with the two preservation results above.
The one ingredient that is not definitional is the transition-level correspondence.
See \leanmodule{Leslie2/Results}.

\subsection{The transition-level correspondence}

A step of $\mathcal{D}(sys_A^w)$ out of a distribution $\mu$ is a hyper-step of $sys_A^w$, and it has to be identified with a single weak transition of $sys_A$ out of $\mu$.
Source decomposition, source mixing and target convexity are the three families that identification rests on, and the bridge lemma is their consequence.

\section{Case study: Asynchronous Byzantine Agreement}

This section verifies an implementation of Asynchronous Byzantine Agreement against a small specification, by probabilistic forward simulation, on the five results proved above.
The setting is $n$ processes, each holding an input bit, of which at most $f$ are Byzantine, with $3f < n$, communicating by asynchronous message passing.
The development lives in the opt-in Lake library \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols}.

\subsection{Setting and conventions}

Three protocols occur in the case study, each given as properties over the calls and returns of its interface that speak only of processes never corrupted.
What this development establishes is Validity and Agreement for ABA, machine-checked; Termination, Unpredictability and fairness are out of scope, and the weak common coin is held at specification level, so $\varepsilon$-Goodness enters as an assumption.

Asynchronous Byzantine Agreement (ABA) takes a bit from each process and may return a bit.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Validity: a correct process returns only a bit a correct process had as input.
  \item Agreement: two correct returns carry the same bit.
  \item $\varepsilon$-sure Termination: once $n-f$ correct processes take part, all correct processes return with probability $1-\varepsilon$.
\end{itemize}

Graded Binding Crusader Agreement (GBCA) takes a bit and may return a graded value in $\{(0,A),\ (0,B),\ (\bot,C),\ (1,B),\ (1,A)\}$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Validity: a correct process failing to return $(b,A)$ implies a correct process had $1-b$ as input.
  \item Graded Agreement: returns $(b,X)$ and $(b',X')$ of correct processes satisfy $b = 0 \Rightarrow b' \neq 1$ and $X = A \Rightarrow X' \neq \bot$.
  \item Binding: when a correct process returns, a bound value $b$ is defined and no extension of the execution returns $(1-b,B)$ or $(1-b,A)$ to a correct process.
  \item Termination: once $n-f$ correct processes take part, all correct processes return.
\end{itemize}

Weak Common Coin (WCC) takes no input and may return a bit.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\varepsilon$-Goodness: with probability $\varepsilon$ all correct processes return $0$, and with probability $\varepsilon$ all return $1$.
  \item Unpredictability: all outcomes remain possible until $f+1$ processes have called.
  \item $(1-\failmass)$-sure Termination: once $n-f$ correct processes take part, all correct processes return with probability $1-\failmass$.
\end{itemize}

Every protocol with sub-protocols is treated in two moves: substitution, in which each sub-implementation is exchanged for the corresponding specification in a fixed context and which is sound by precongruence, and the core simulation, in which the core algorithm beside the specifications of its sub-protocols is simulated by the protocol's own specification.
Composing the two inclusions places the implementation inside the specification, and the recursion stops at GBCA, which calls no sub-protocol, and at WCC, which is left at specification level.

\paragraph{Object names.}
Transition System~1 is the ABA specification, Transition System~2 the GBCA specification and Transition System~3 the WCC specification; the three names are used throughout this section and in every node of it.
A rule is cited by its Lean constructor name, a deviation label in parentheses marks a point where an encoding departs from~\cite{LeslieBP}, and where a node names ABDY22's Algorithm~$k$ it means the numbering of~\cite{ABDY22}.

\paragraph{Deviations.}
Each departure from~\cite{LeslieBP} carries a label D1--D19, cited at the point where it applies; the labels D2, D6 and D7 are unused.
\begin{itemize}
  \item D1: corruption is a total deterministic transform.
  \item D3: the re-propose guard on \leandeclnamed{repropose}{PLTS.ABA.SpecStep.repropose} of Transition System~1.
  \item D4: the WCC \texttt{guess} label and the \texttt{guess} state field are omitted.
  \item D5: a set-based authenticated network with per-sender message pools.
  \item D8: protocol sends gated on prior participation.
  \item D9: $0$-based round indexing.
  \item D10: the per-round bookkeeping between the coin return and the next call fused into the $retW$ step, which multicasts DECIDED on an $A$ grade.
  \item D11: Byzantine call- and return-driver rules at each handshake.
  \item D12$'$: per-process DECIDED sent and received pools, delivery per (receiver, sender, bit), and Byzantine equivocation at that layer.
  \item D13: the Validity provenance apparatus on Transition System~1, namely a ghost record of genuine calls, an $f+1$ support guard on the mixed-bind rule, a provenance guard on re-proposal, and a Byzantine fill rule.
  \item D14: $f+1$ support guards on the GBCA specification's binding, on the dissenting bit at its $B$-return, and on each of the two bits at its $C$-return.
  \item D15: the $F$-blind form of those support counts, namely $f+1$ distinct callers-or-corrupted.
  \item D16: the two-phase abstract twin used by the core simulation.
  \item D17: a fourth coin outcome $\dagger$, of mass $\failmass$, on the single distribution both coin resolutions follow, which delivers to no process and freezes the round it resolves.
  \item D18: the GBCA implementation transcribes ABDY22's Algorithm~6 in full, six rounds over the message levels INPUT, ECHO, VOTE, BIND and SEAL, in place of the source's four-round compression of it, which elides the SEAL level and takes $f{+}1$ $\mathrm{VOTE}\,v$ receipts as the grade-$1$ witness and thereby violates Binding.
  \item D19: the GBCA specification state carries an exclusion set $dead \subseteq \{0,1\}$, the bits the instance is barred from handing out, in place of the source's bound value $bind \in \{0,1,\bot\}$, which makes Binding negative and monotone.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Outside the model.}
Two features of~\cite{LeslieBP} are deliberately not encoded: fairness, whose markings bear only on liveness, and partial information, whose observation functions and belief construction are not needed because every adversary here is omniscient.

\paragraph{Notation.}
Write $[n]$ for the identifier set and $F \subseteq [n]$ for the corrupted set.
Values range over $\{0,1,\bot,\top,\dagger\}$, the type \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.TVal}, where $\bot$ is unresolved, $\top$ is an adversarially resolved outcome and $\dagger$ is a resolution that delivers to no process (D17); the cross-domain equality \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.TVal.agrees} holds of $\bot$ with $\bot$ and of $b$ with $b$ alone.

\subsection{The graded-agreement stage}

The graded-agreement layer is the one sub-protocol carried to implementation level, and its messages form a five-level ladder.
The depth of the ladder is what the grade-$1$ evidence buys (D18): $f{+}1$ $\mathrm{BIND}\,v$ receipts exceed the corruption budget, so an honest sender stands behind every grade-$1$ output, and that sender's own wait condition is the $n{-}f$ $\mathrm{VOTE}\,v$ quorum the binding argument of~\cite{ABDY22} counts.
The definition below regroups the layer as one per-round PLTS over the whole system state, which is the form Theorem~\ref{thm:gbca-inst-refines} is proved against; its guards are local although its state is global, and Definitions~\ref{def:aba-procnet} and~\ref{def:aba-netadv} are where each of them is written against one node.
See \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/GBCAImpl}.

\subsection{The round loop}

The round loop is the core algorithm of ABA, and it is the layer that calls the other two.
Its external return is gated on an $n-f$ DECIDED quorum, and that count is what excludes disagreement: two returns of different bits would need two sender sets of size $n-f$, and any two such sets meet in an honest process.

\subsection{The ABA specification and its safety}

The three specification systems locate the adversary differently from the implementations they stand for.
An implementation pairs corruption-blind processes with an explicit network-adversary PLTS, which owns the corrupted set together with its budget and authorises every Byzantine action by the guard $k \in F$ of a component; a specification carries no network component at all, and its adversarial transitions are bare nondeterministic choices resolved by the scheduler.
Leaving those choices unstructured is sound because a specification constrains from above: an unstructured adversary makes the specification more permissive, never less, and trace-distribution inclusion asks only that every behaviour of the implementation be matched by some resolution of the specification's nondeterminism.

Transition System~1 is ten rules over the record \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.SpecState}, and its coin rule is the only probabilistic transition of the system.
Safety is proved by induction over those ten rules under two invariants, one making the decision value write-once and one carrying the provenance of each bind and each decision back to an $F$-blind support; the budget pigeonhole then leaves one supporter never corrupted and ghost-recorded as having called the value, which is the caller Validity names.
See \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/Spec} and \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/SpecSafety}.

\subsection{The sub-protocol specifications}

Transition Systems~2 and~3 are the specifications of the two sub-protocols, given as round-indexed instances together with the $\mathbb{N}$-indexed families the round loop calls.
The GBCA instance, over \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.SpecState}, binds by removing an option rather than by choosing one (D19): its exclusion set starts empty, has the internal binding transition as its only writer, and grows along every execution, which is what makes Graded Agreement and Binding structural rather than invariants.

\subsection{The GBCA instance refinement}

The graded-agreement layer is carried to implementation level, and the work that carries it is done once, on one round instance.
The relation reads the specification's call slots, returns and corrupted set straight off the implementation state, and certifies the two fields the protocol does not store, $dead$ and $grade$, by receipt patterns; a kill is fired on demand, as a two-step weak burst under a return that needs it, which is why the obligation is discharged by a weak transition rather than by a single step.
This is the only refinement of the case study proved against protocol code, and everything downstream of it is transport.
See \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/GBCASim}.

\subsection{From the deployment-shaped specification to the ABA specification}

The proof repartitions state onto a monolithic composition and runs a hand-built simulation from there.
Assembling $n$ nodes and a network into one record loses the distinction between states that differ only in how the same data is distributed, which is why the repartition is an inclusion and not an equality.
The simulation's abstract twin is lazy and two-phase (D16), deferring the decision to a single weak step under the first visible return, which is what a forward simulation permits and a strong one does not.
See \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/Hybrid} and \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/CoreSim}.

\subsection{The main theorems}

Three steps separate the deployed protocol from the ABA specification:
\[
  \mathrm{deployed}(P)
  \;\overset{\text{(1)}}{=}\; \mathrm{layered}(P)
  \;\overset{\text{(2)}}{\sqsubseteq}\; \mathrm{layeredSpec}(P)
  \;\overset{\text{(3)}}{\sqsubseteq}\; \mathrm{spec}(P),
\]
where (1) is the regrouping of Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-layered-atd}, exact in both directions; (2) the substitution of Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-substitution}; and (3) the refinement of Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-layeredspec-spec}, whose proof repartitions state onto the monolithic composition and applies the core simulation there.
Safety then transfers by Theorem~\ref{thm:trace-support}, with no hypothesis on the trace beyond positive probability, and both properties are stated on all returns, corrupted returners included.
The chain is assembled in \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/Main}.

\subsection{Non-vacuity}

A refinement statement is compatible with a system that does nothing.
The chain is therefore accompanied by machine-checked witnesses that the composition the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-layeredspec-spec} passes through executes and reaches a return at $n = 4$ and $f = 1$, on a connected run of positive probability that exercises the one probabilistic transition on the way, and that the $\mathrm{fail}$ label is live in that composition rather than merely declared.
Membership of a decision trace in the achievable trace distributions of the deployed protocol is not established; the witnesses are about that composition alone.
See \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/Examples}.
